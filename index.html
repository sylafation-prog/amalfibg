The absence year

What six months away does to a house

Most waterfront houses in South Florida stand empty from May to October. Nothing is being neglected on purpose — the owner is simply somewhere else, and the Florida year carries on without them.

January

In residence

Dry season. The lowest humidity of the year, and the only stretch the house gets a rest.

February

In residence

Full house. Every system running at once, and nothing hidden for long.

March

In residence

Pollen and the first real heat. Filters load faster than anyone expects.

April

In residence

The last cool nights of the year. Irrigation demand starts to climb.

May

Closed

The house closes. From here, nothing moves unless someone moves it.

June

Closed

Hurricane season opens. The air inside stops moving, and the thermostat alone will not dry it.

July

Closed

Humidity past sixty per cent. Mold begins on the coolest surfaces — behind casework, inside exterior walls.

August

Closed

Peak salt load. Condenser coils, fasteners, rails and hinges take the worst of the year.

September

Closed

The storm that missed still drove salt across everything. Debris sits in the drains.

October

Closed

The first dry air arrives, and whatever happened over the summer is now visible. And now expensive.

November

Returning

The owner returns. The list begins, and every item on it is older than it looks.

December

In residence

Repairs that should have been maintenance.

Our standard

Finished means finished

Amalfi was founded by a European-born operator who spent twenty years owning and managing commercial buildings, and renovating them with his own hands. The standard follows from that: a job is done when the edges are right, not when the invoice is ready.

Written before it begins

Every property receives an assessment and a schedule in writing before a crew is sent. You know what is to be done, when, and at what cost. Changes are agreed in advance, never discovered afterwards.

The same faces each time

One named steward to a property. The people who arrive know where the shutoffs are, which unit failed last August and which gate code works. Continuity is the service.

Shown when it is done

Photographs and a written record of each visit, sent to your telephone. You see the work whether or not you were on the property that day.

The European standard

Specified for fifty years, not for the warranty period

In Europe a tradesman qualifies under a master before he is allowed to work unsupervised, tile and stone are set to tolerances written in millimetres, and a building is detailed on the assumption that somebody will still be maintaining it in fifty years rather than replacing it in fifteen. That is the habit we build with here.

It is not an affectation on this coast. The Amalfi Coast and South Florida face the same three adversaries — salt, hard sun and masonry that moves — and one of them has been answering them for several centuries longer. What it means on your property is a tighter tolerance, a longer-lived specification, and details built to be opened and serviced instead of torn out.
