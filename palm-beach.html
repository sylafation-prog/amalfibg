Estate Care in Palm Beach

A barrier island with the Atlantic on one side and the lagoon on the other, so the salt arrives from both directions. Half the houses are shut for half the year. Over three hundred of them are landmarked. And the Town has firm views about when work may be done and how loud it may be. Working here is less a question of capability than of knowing all of that before you arrive.

On the island

What we hold in Palm Beach

Amalfi Estates keeps private residences on the island to a written calendar of care, under one named steward and a single monthly statement. Dispatch runs at every hour, with a four-hour emergency target in Palm Beach County, and every engagement begins with a survey at no cost.

The Estate Section and South Ocean

Ocean-to-lake properties with the most severe exposure in the county — spray on the east elevations, lagoon humidity on the west, and the greatest concentration of Mizner, Wyeth, Fatio and Volk houses anywhere in Florida.

The North End

Closer-set houses, heavy seasonal turnover and a stock that is largely post-war and much remodelled. Less landmark fabric, more mechanical plant, and owners who arrive for a season and expect the house ready on the day.

Midtown, Worth Avenue and the vias

Residential and commercial interleaved, with the tightest working-hour and noise constraints on the island. Scheduling matters here more than anywhere else we work.

Everglades Island, Ibis Isle and the lakefront

Dock, lift and seawall exposure on the lagoon side. Calmer water than the ocean elevations, and consequently far heavier biological growth on pilings, hulls and the north faces of everything.

Local conditions

The Town decides when the work happens

More jobs go wrong on this island for scheduling reasons than for technical ones. A contractor who turns up on a Saturday in February with a gas machine is not a contractor you will be asked to recommend. These are the constraints we plan around, and we confirm them against the Town rather than from memory, because the dates move.

The season is a working restriction, not just a social one. From roughly the start of November — the Monday before Thanksgiving, for landscaping — through to the end of April, work on the island is a weekday proposition. Construction is prohibited on Saturdays, Sundays and Town-observed holidays, and shuts entirely from Christmas Eve to New Year's Day. Crews may not arrive on site before half past seven, and lawn maintenance may make no noise before nine.

Specific machinery is barred in season. Under the Town's noise provisions, dry sandblasters, pneumatic jackhammers, pile drivers, dredges and gasoline, diesel or steam engines that give off offensive noise or odour are prohibited during the season and at weekends and holidays. A loud petrol pressure washer sits squarely in the spirit of that list. It is the reason exterior washing on this island is scheduled into the shoulder months and the permitted weekday window rather than fitted in whenever a crew has a gap — and the reason we run the quietest equipment we can buy.

The landscape rules catch almost everybody. Gasoline leaf blowers are prohibited on properties under one acre. No blower may exceed 65 dBA measured at fifty feet. Blowing clippings into a public street is unlawful. A great many crews working the island breach at least one of those every week, and the consequence arrives at the homeowner rather than at the crew. Directing the landscape contractor to a written standard is one of the quieter things we do here, and on this island it is one of the most valuable.

And the right of way is not yours. A bucket truck, a lift or a scaffold in the road or across the sidewalk needs a Right-of-Way Permit. Working in the right of way without one, or storing material there, draws a minimum fine per day and per vehicle — $150 for a first offence, $250 for a second and $500 for each one after that.

How we plan a Palm Beach year

  • Loud exterior work placed in May to October wherever the calendar allows
  • In-season work kept to the permitted weekday window
  • Nothing scheduled for Saturdays, Sundays or Town holidays in season
  • No work booked between Christmas Eve and New Year's Day
  • Arrival times staged so nobody is parked on the street early
  • Quietest available equipment, and electric where it exists
  • Right-of-Way Permit pulled before any lift or scaffold
  • Landscape crews held to the blower and decibel rules
  • Dates confirmed with the Town each season, not assumed

Landmarks

Three hundred and twenty-eight reasons to be careful

The Town protects over 328 landmark properties, sites and vistas under its Historic Preservation Ordinance. A seven-member Landmarks Preservation Commission, six of whom must be residents, meets monthly on the third Wednesday to review proposed changes and issue Certificates of Appropriateness, and it administers the Town's tax abatement programme.

Routine maintenance and repair in kind generally sits outside that review — which is why relaying original tile is treated so differently from replacing it. Anything that changes material, configuration or appearance generally does not. Keeping a house firmly on the maintenance side of that line is cheaper than the alternative and needs nobody's permission — which is the whole argument for a written calendar on a landmarked property.

Lime stucco cleaned at the wrong pressure, a cement patch over soft render, a film-forming sealer on a breathing wall, or another season of shutter anchors drilled into cast stone will each do permanent harm to one of these houses, and all four are done here every year by competent people who meant well.

Exposure

Salt from both sides

On most of this coast a house has a weather side and a sheltered side. On a barrier island a quarter of a mile across it has neither. Ocean spray works the east elevations and lagoon humidity works the west, and a property closed from Easter to Thanksgiving spends the hardest half of the year doing it unattended. Humidity does more damage to an empty Florida house than occupancy ever does, which is why the calendar does not pause when the owner leaves.

Questions

What Palm Beach owners ask first

When are you actually allowed to work on the island?

In season — roughly November through April — work is a weekday proposition. Construction is prohibited on Saturdays, Sundays and Town-observed holidays, and nothing runs between Christmas Eve and New Year's Day. Crews may not arrive before half past seven, and lawn maintenance may make no noise before nine. Outside season the window widens and quiet Saturday work becomes possible. The Town sets these hours and revises them, so we confirm each season rather than working from last year's sheet.

Can you pressure wash a Palm Beach house during the season?

Carefully, quietly, and on a weekday. The Town's noise provisions bar a list of equipment during season and at weekends that includes gasoline, diesel and steam engines giving off offensive noise or odour, and a loud petrol washer sits squarely in the spirit of it. We plan the loud exterior work into the shoulder months wherever the calendar allows, keep in-season washing to the permitted weekday window, and run the quietest equipment available. On a historic house there is a second reason to go gently: high pressure destroys lime stucco. That is covered on the historic and landmark page.

Do we need approval to have a landmarked house maintained?

Generally no. The Town reserves the Certificate of Appropriateness for work that changes material, configuration or appearance, and routine maintenance and repair in kind normally sits outside that review. Where the line falls for a particular property is a question for the Town, and we will always say so rather than guess. What we can do is keep the house on the maintenance side of the line, which is both cheaper and faster than the alternative.

Our landscape crew uses gas blowers. Is that a problem?

It may well be. Gasoline leaf blowers are prohibited on Palm Beach properties under one acre, no blower may exceed 65 dBA at fifty feet, and blowing clippings into a public street is unlawful. Crews breach these regularly and the consequence reaches the homeowner, not the crew. Under grounds oversight we hold your existing landscape contractor to a written standard, which includes the Town's equipment rules.

Do you charge for the survey?

No. Every engagement begins with a survey at no cost and the written report is yours whether or not you engage us. For a residence that means a walk of the exterior, the systems and the waterfront structures, photographed and written up with a recommended calendar of care. On a historic house it extends to the fabric record: materials identified surface by surface, existing patches and coatings flagged, crack lines traced and dated, and every anchor in the historic fabric located.

Will you work alongside our architect, designer and existing contractors?

That is the normal arrangement. Most houses on this island already have good professionals; what is usually missing is somebody at the property regularly enough to notice things between their visits. We keep the record, do the ordinary work, and call the specialists when the house needs what only they can do. Nobody is replaced and nobody is cut out.
