On the Mile

What we hold in Hillsboro Beach

Amalfi Estates keeps private residences on the Hillsboro Mile to a written calendar of care, under one named steward and a single monthly statement. Dispatch runs at every hour, with a four-hour emergency target in Broward County, and every engagement begins with a survey at no cost.

Ocean-to-Intracoastal estates

The signature property here: beach frontage on one side, a dock and lift on the other, and a house in between taking salt from both. Two sets of waterfront structures and two sets of problems on a single title.

Ocean-side houses and the beachfront

Direct Atlantic exposure with no barrier of any kind. Spray reaches glass, metal and finishes daily, not occasionally, and the east elevation of a house here ages at several times the rate of the west.

Intracoastal-side houses and docks

Calmer water, far heavier biological growth. Pilings, lifts, seawall caps and the north faces of everything need a different cycle from the ocean side of the same street.

The low-rise condominiums

Older association buildings along the Mile with shared plant, common-area glass and walkways, salt-exposed railings and garages, and a board that wants one accountable party rather than six.

Local conditions

A town that is mostly water, and mostly empty

Two facts about Hillsboro Beach decide how a property here should be looked after, and both of them come straight out of the census.

The first is that the town is barely land. Of the roughly one and a half square miles inside the town limits, only about a third of a square mile is dry. The rest is Atlantic and Intracoastal. That geometry is the whole maintenance brief: there is no inland buffer, no sheltered elevation, and no part of a property here that is not within a few dozen yards of salt water. Glass, hardware, metalwork, coatings, air conditioning condensers and anything with a bearing or a hinge in it all run on a shorter clock than the same item would a mile west.

The second is that most of these houses are shut. At the 2020 census, 51.9 per cent of the town's 2,428 housing units were vacant, and the population roughly doubles in winter. Half the Mile is a closed house for half the year, and a closed house in this climate is not a house at rest. Humidity does more damage to an unoccupied Florida property than occupancy ever does: the air handler short-cycles, traps dry out, condensate lines block without anyone noticing, and the icemaker drips into a cabinet from April to Thanksgiving.

And there is only the one road. A1A through the town is a single lane each way with no shoulder and very little parking, which makes staging a real consideration rather than an afterthought. Crews arrive together, park once, and work the property rather than running back and forth, because on this street a van left in the wrong place is a problem for every neighbour on it.

The median age in the town is approaching sixty-seven, and more than half of residents are sixty-five or over. A good many of the owners here are not looking for a project. They want the house handled, by the same people, on a schedule, with one invoice and somebody who answers the phone.

What a Hillsboro Mile property needs

  • Fresh-water salt rinsing on both elevations, on a cycle and after onshore blows
  • Glass watched for etching on the ocean side, where it starts first
  • Separate cycles for the ocean and Intracoastal faces of the same house
  • Condenser and coil care specified for a marine environment
  • Dehumidification held through the closed months, not switched off
  • Pilings, lift, seawall cap and dock decking on a watched cycle
  • Biological growth managed on the lagoon side year-round
  • Storm close, shutter programme and post-blow rinse
  • Opening and closing of the house either side of the season
  • One statement, one steward, and crews that stage once

Two waterfronts

The same house, twice over

An ocean-to-Intracoastal property is not one waterfront house, it is two, and they fail differently. The Atlantic side is abrasive: wind-driven salt and sand, ultraviolet, etched glass, pitted hardware, coatings that give out years early. The Intracoastal side is biological: growth on pilings and hulls, damp that never fully dries, and a seawall quietly doing the most important job on the property. A single maintenance schedule applied evenly across both is a schedule that over-serves one and neglects the other.

Questions

What Hillsboro Beach owners ask first

How quickly do you respond to an emergency on the Hillsboro Mile?

Four hours, day or night. Dispatch runs at every hour, and clients under an agreement are attended first and pay no overtime premium. Targets for every county are set out on the coverage page.

We are only here for the season. What happens the rest of the year?

That is the half of the year that decides what condition you walk into. Over half the housing in this town sits vacant and the population roughly doubles in winter, so most of the Mile is closed at exactly the time the climate is hardest on it. Under a programme the calendar does not pause: dehumidification is held rather than switched off, the house is walked and photographed, the water is run, the mechanical plant is serviced, and the property is opened properly before you arrive rather than discovered on the day.

Our house runs from the ocean to the Intracoastal. Is that one property or two?

For maintenance purposes it is two. The Atlantic elevation is an abrasive environment — wind-driven salt and sand, ultraviolet, glass that etches, hardware that pits, coatings that fail early. The Intracoastal side is a biological one — growth on pilings and hulls, damp that never fully dries, and a seawall carrying the property. We run them on separate cycles, because a single schedule applied evenly across both will over-serve one and neglect the other.

Do you look after the dock, the lift and the seawall?

Yes, as a watched cycle rather than a call-out. Pilings, lift mechanism and cables, seawall cap and face, dock decking and hardware are all inspected visually, photographed from fixed positions and recorded. Structural marine work goes to a licensed marine contractor under permit, contracting directly with you. The point of the cycle is that the failure is found in the month it starts rather than the year it finishes.

Parking and access on A1A are difficult. How do you handle that?

Deliberately. There is one road through the town, a single lane each way, no shoulder and very little parking. Crews arrive together, stage once on the property, and work it through rather than coming and going. Lifts or scaffolding in the right of way are permitted before they appear, not after a neighbour calls.

Do you charge for the survey?

No. Every engagement begins with a survey at no cost and the written report is yours whether or not you engage us — a walk of the exterior, the systems and the waterfront structures, photographed and written up with a recommended calendar of care.
