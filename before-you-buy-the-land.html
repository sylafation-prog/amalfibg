Building here · Step one

The decision before all the other decisions

By the time most owners speak to anyone about building, the land is bought. Which is unfortunate, because the parcel settles more about the house than the architect will — how high the floor sits, what the openings cost, which months the site may be worked, whether there is a seawall obligation attached, and how long the whole thing takes. None of that is visible from the listing, and all of it is knowable before you offer.

Why it matters here

Two parcels, a mile apart, are not the same project

On this coast the variation between adjacent neighbourhoods is larger than the variation between most American cities, and almost none of it is priced into the land.

An owner comparing two waterfront lots will usually compare frontage, orientation, water depth and price. Those are the right questions and they are nowhere near sufficient. Two parcels a mile apart can sit in different flood zones, on different sides of the county line that separates the high-velocity hurricane zone from the rest of Florida, under different municipal working seasons, with one carrying a seawall at four feet and the other a wall with a compliance obligation and a sixty-day clock attached to it.

The differences are not marginal. The flood datum decides the finished floor elevation, which decides the entry sequence, the driveway, the relationship between the house and the garden, and whether the ground floor can hold habitable rooms at all. A parcel in Miami-Dade or Broward is in the high-velocity hurricane zone and every opening becomes a tested assembly carrying documented approval, which adds something in the order of eight to twelve per cent before anyone has chosen a finish. A parcel in a barrier-island town with a defined construction season may not be worked on weekdays outside it, and in some towns the loudest categories of work — pile driving, concrete pumping — are prohibited outright rather than restricted, which means the structure either goes up before the season or waits until after it.

None of this is obscure. It is published, it is specific to the parcel, and it can be established in a few hours of reading before an offer is made. What makes it invisible is that nobody in the transaction is paid to look. The listing agent sells the land, the buyer's agent sells the purchase, the architect is not yet appointed, and the builder arrives long after the decision is irreversible.

Which is the gap this page exists to describe, and the reason we read parcels for people at no charge. The point in the process where information is worth the most is also the point where nobody is being paid to supply it.

The comparison

Seven things to establish on any parcel, in order

Not a conveyancing checklist. These are the items capable of changing the project rather than delaying it.

  1. Where the flood map puts it, and what the town adds on topThe base flood elevation is the federal number. The municipality usually requires freeboard above it, and the design flood elevation is what you actually build to. Those are three different figures and owners routinely conflate them. On a low parcel the answer determines whether you are building a house or a house on a podium, and it is the first thing that should be read.
  2. Which side of the hurricane-zone line it falls onMiami-Dade and Broward are the high-velocity hurricane zone. Palm Beach County and the Gulf coast are not. The testing standards are different, the approvals are different, and a product approved for one is not automatically acceptable on the other. The cost difference is material and it lands on every opening in the house.
  3. What the town does between November and MaySeveral towns on this coast define a construction season and restrict or suspend work inside it. This is not a noise ordinance in the ordinary sense; it closes the site. A project that ignores it does not run slowly, it stops, and the stop lands in the months an owner most wants progress. Establish the dates, the permitted days and hours, and the categories prohibited outright before the schedule is written.
  4. Whether there is a seawall, and what condition it is inOn a waterfront parcel this is routinely the largest unmodelled number. Broward County sets a minimum top elevation for tidal flood barriers and defines a repair threshold that converts a large repair into a rebuild of the entire shoreline. A wall near the end of its life on a property about to be redeveloped is a cost at the beginning of the project, not the end. We have written about what the rule actually says.
  5. What the zoning envelope really isSetbacks, lot coverage, floor area ratio, height and the datum it is measured from, plus any overlay. On the water, add the waterward setback. This is a reading exercise and it belongs to the buyer, because the answer determines whether the house in your head fits on the land in front of you.
  6. Whether anything is designated, and which board sitsA historic designation or an architectural review requirement adds a body with its own calendar and its own opinions. It is not necessarily a problem — some of the best houses on this coast are in designated districts — but it changes both the programme and the design conversation, and it should not be discovered after the land is bought.
  7. How you get plant onto itNarrow street, overhead lines, a bridge with a weight limit, no staging area, a neighbour's wall eighteen inches from the line. Access is the constraint nobody checks and it shows up in every price. On a tight barrier-island lot the logistics can be a larger cost driver than the finishes.

The architect

The appointment everyone makes first, and the question nobody asks

It is worth knowing that Florida does not require an architect for a house at all. That fact is less useful as a saving than as a way of working out what you are actually buying.

Section 481.229(1)(b) of the Florida Statutes exempts “any one-family or two-family residence building, townhouse, or domestic outbuilding” appurtenant to one from the requirement that plans be prepared and sealed by a registered architect — and it adds the words regardless of cost. A large waterfront house is, in the eyes of Chapter 481, the same category as a modest one. Subsection (1)(c) separately exempts any other building costing less than $25,000.

So the architect's seal is not what gets a house through plan review here. What does is a complete document set the building official will accept: sealed structural engineering, product approvals for every opening and assembly, energy calculations, and flood design documentation in a flood zone. Much of that is the engineer's work rather than the architect's.

The useful consequence is not that you should skip the architect. On a house of any ambition that is usually a false economy, and the saving comes back as change orders against an incomplete set. The consequence is that you now know what the fee buys: design and coordination, not permission. And that lets you ask the right question when you interview, which is not whether they are licensed but whether they have built on this coast.

The difference shows quickly. An architect who has built here designs the ground floor around the flood datum rather than discovering it, knows that every opening is a tested assembly and specifies accordingly, has read a barrier-island town's working calendar and sequences the structure around it, and does not present a scheme before the survey exists. One who has not will produce something beautiful that gets dismantled at the second zoning meeting, and the owner pays for both versions.

We will say which questions to put to an architect, and we will introduce people we have seen work. We will not tell you who to hire, and nobody standing to gain from the answer should.

What to ask an architect here

  • How many houses have you completed in this municipality
  • How do you handle the finished floor elevation on a low parcel
  • Who does your structural engineering, and how early
  • How do you specify openings for product approval
  • Have you presented to this town's review board
  • What do you do before the survey exists
  • Who coordinates the consultants, and is that in your fee
  • At what stage do you want a builder pricing the set

Why the sequence is the thing that saves money

Almost every owner begins with the design, because design is the part people have heard of. It is not usually the right first call.

The step most often reversed is the first one, and the step most often skipped is the builder. A builder reading drawings at fifty per cent completion can price the structure, flag the details that cost disproportionately and tell you whether the house on the page is the house in the budget. A builder reading finished drawings can only tell you how much more it is than you thought, at the moment it is most expensive to change. The hiring order works through each appointment in turn.

Questions

What owners ask

How do I know if a lot is buildable before I make an offer?

Establish seven things, and all of them are knowable in a few hours: the flood zone and what the municipality adds above the base flood elevation; whether the parcel sits in the high-velocity hurricane zone, which is Miami-Dade and Broward only; the town's construction season and what it prohibits; the seawall, its elevation and any compliance obligation; the zoning envelope of setbacks, coverage, floor area ratio and height with its datum; whether anything is designated or sits in an overlay; and how plant gets onto the site. None of it is obscure and none of it is in the listing. We do this at no charge because it is the point where information is worth the most.

Do I need an architect to build a house in Florida?

No. Section 481.229(1)(b) exempts one-family and two-family residence buildings, townhouses and appurtenant domestic outbuildings from the requirement for an architect's seal, regardless of cost. What the building official requires instead is a complete document set — sealed structural engineering, product approvals for every opening, energy calculations and, in a flood zone, flood design documentation. The exemption removes a legal requirement, not the technical work. On a significant house hiring an architect is usually still right, but you are buying design and coordination rather than permission, and you should judge the fee on that.

Who should I appoint first?

The surveyor, before anything is designed. Boundary and topographic survey, a tree survey where there is canopy, and an elevation certificate in a flood zone. Those documents define the buildable envelope, the ground, the protected trees and the required finished floor, and anything designed before they exist is designed on assumption. Zoning research belongs in the same phase, soil borings follow immediately on anything substantial, and the designer comes after, with the constraints in hand.

Can you help me find land?

We read the parcels you or your agent are already considering and tell you which of them will build, what each would cost and what each would take. Finding parcels and handling the transaction is your agent's work and we take no part in it — which is why the two sit together well. They bring you the options; we tell you what each one really is.

What is the difference between two lots a mile apart?

Potentially a year of calendar and a seven-figure obligation. They can sit in different flood zones, which changes the finished floor and therefore the whole ground floor; on different sides of the line that separates the high-velocity hurricane zone from the rest of Florida, which changes every opening in the house; under different municipal working seasons, one of which may close the site for months; and one may carry a seawall with a compliance obligation attached while the other does not. None of that appears in a price per square foot of land.

Will you tell me which architect to use?

We will tell you what to ask one, and we will introduce people we have seen work, without taking anything for the introduction. We will not tell you who to hire. The questions that matter here are not about qualifications: how many houses they have completed in this municipality, how they handle the finished floor elevation on a low parcel, who does their structural engineering and how early, how they specify openings for product approval, and whether they will have a builder pricing the set before it is finished.

What does the parcel review cost?

Nothing, and there is no obligation attached to it. Send an address. We come back with the flood position, the zoning envelope, the overlays, the seawall if there is one, the town's working season as a calendar, the access constraints we can see, and the documents worth pulling. If the parcel is wrong for what you have in mind we will say so, which is the most useful thing we can tell you and the cheapest moment to hear it.
