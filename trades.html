Licensing

What needs a licence here, and what does not

Owners are rarely told this clearly, and it is the question we are asked most often once somebody realises we draw the line in writing. Florida is unusually explicit about it.

Florida licenses construction contracting under Chapter 489, and the categories it licenses are specific: general and building contracting, roofing, mechanical and air conditioning, plumbing, electrical, pools, solar and a handful of others. A great many trades were never in that list, and until recently those were licensed locally instead, county by county, with a certificate of competency.

That system was dismantled. A sequence of statutes — HB 735 in 2021, HB 1383 in 2023 and SB 1142 in 2024 — preempted local occupational licensing to the state, and Fla. Stat. 489.117(4)(a) now names the exempt job scopes outright: painting; flooring; cabinetry; interior remodeling when the scope of the project does not include a task for which a state license is required; driveway or tennis court installation; handyman services; decorative stone, tile, marble, granite, or terrazzo installation; plastering; pressure washing; stuccoing; caulking; and canvas awning and ornamental iron installation. The same provision states that a local government may not require a licence in order to obtain a permit for those scopes.

Miami-Dade stopped issuing certificates of competency for its preempted categories in 2023. Palm Beach County ended its painting certificate on 1 July 2025 and reclassified the holders. The practical effect is that most of what we do requires a business tax receipt and current insurance rather than a trade licence.

We say so plainly because the alternative is worse. A firm that is vague about this is usually vague because part of what it is selling sits on the wrong side of the line, and the consequence of that lands on the owner: Florida makes a contract entered into by an unlicensed contractor unenforceable, and an uninsured or improperly licensed trade on your property is your exposure rather than theirs.

So every page here carries a section setting out exactly where that trade stops. Where a job needs licensed work — roofing, mechanical, plumbing, electrical, structural, window replacement, mould above the statutory threshold — the licensed trade contracts directly with you and you see the licence and the certificate of insurance before anyone arrives. Florida’s statute draws no distinction between performing licensed work yourself and having it performed by others under your contract, which is precisely why we keep it out of ours.

Three things worth knowing

  • Preemption is not universal. A county containing an area of critical state concern may still licence these scopes if it did so before January 2021. In South Florida that means Monroe County and the Keys.
  • Three carve-outs survive. A local government may continue to licence veneer work — gutters, siding, soffit and fascia — rooftop painting, coating and cleaning above three storeys, and fence installation, where it required it before January 2021.
  • A permit is still a permit. Preemption removed the trade licence, not the building code. Work that needs a permit still needs one, and work inside a state-licensed category still needs that licence however it is described on an invoice.

None of this is legal advice and we are not lawyers. It is the basis on which we decide what to take and what to hand over, set out so that you can check it against the statute rather than take our word for it.

Together

Most of these are cheaper as a calendar than as a job

Painting, sealants, stone and metal all fail slowly, predictably and in a sequence. Caught at the start of that sequence each is a morning’s work; caught at the end, each is a project. The estate calendar exists to catch them at the start, and it is why these trades sit inside a maintenance programme rather than waiting for the telephone to ring.
