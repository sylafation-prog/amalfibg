Town of Highland Beach · Four hours, day or night

Estate Care in Highland Beach

A town of about four thousand three hundred people with four thousand three hundred housing units — roughly one for every resident, and forty-four per cent of them empty on census day. The median age is approaching seventy and three in five residents are over sixty-five. Almost nothing here is a year-round, fully occupied house, and that single fact should determine how a property in this town is looked after.

In town

What we hold in Highland Beach

Amalfi keeps private residences and condominium common property in Highland Beach to a written calendar of care, under one named steward and a single monthly statement. Dispatch runs at every hour, with a four-hour emergency target in Palm Beach County, and every engagement begins with a survey at no cost.

Oceanfront condominium associations

The dominant building type. Shared plant, common-area glass and walkways, salt-exposed railings, garages and pool decks, and a board that wants one accountable party rather than six separate vendors with six separate stories.

Single-family on the Intracoastal

Houses on the lagoon side with docks, lifts and seawalls, generally quieter water than the ocean elevations and consequently much heavier biological growth on pilings and north faces.

Ocean-to-Intracoastal properties

Where a lot runs the width of the strip, the property carries two waterfronts: an abrasive Atlantic elevation and a biological lagoon one. They want separate maintenance cycles, not one schedule applied evenly.

Seasonally closed residences

With over forty per cent of the town’s housing standing empty, most of what we do here happens with nobody home — dehumidification held rather than switched off, the house walked and photographed, and the property opened properly before the owner lands.

Local conditions

The Town publishes a handbook, and it is specific

Highland Beach sets out its construction site rules in a published Construction Site Management Handbook drawn from Chapter 12 of the Town Code. Very few towns do, and the detail in it goes a long way past working hours.

DayWhat is permitted
WeekdaysWork prohibited before 8:00am or after 6:00pm.
Saturdays9:00am to 3:00pm only — and a restricted day even inside that window.
SundaysAll construction prohibited.
HolidaysProhibited on New Year’s Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas.
ArrivalWorkers may not arrive more than thirty minutes before permitted hours, or leave more than thirty minutes after.

The Saturday restriction is the one worth reading twice. Section 12-6(b) does not merely shorten Saturday — it removes most of what makes a Saturday useful. No noise disturbance across the property line, and specifically no placing, cleaning or removing of dumpsters or portable restrooms, no concrete delivery or pumping, no dump trucks, cranes or bulldozers, and no jackhammering. What is left is quiet hand work. Any vendor who books a Saturday here for a delivery or a dumpster swap has booked a violation.

The noise rule has no number in it. Section 12-7 sets no decibel limit at all; the test is whether the noise annoys, disturbs, injures or endangers the comfort, repose, health, peace or safety of a considerable number of people. That is a judgement standard rather than a measurable one, which in practice makes it stricter rather than looser — there is no reading you can point to in your defence. The same section prohibits contractors, employees and agents from playing music at the site at any time.

Access on A1A is actively managed. On-street parking is prohibited and every vehicle belongs on site or at an approved alternate. Parking on A1A itself requires an FDOT permit, which must be given to the Town before building permits are released. Obstructing traffic for more than three minutes requires a flag person in a reflective vest; more than five minutes requires advance police approval and an officer on site. Trucks over nine tons loaded are discouraged and need twenty-four hours’ notice to Police if unavoidable, and cranes need two hours’ notice and a police escort.

Hours, holidays and the handbook are the Town’s to set and to revise. We confirm them with the Building Department each season rather than working from a previous copy. The Town also notes that the handbook does not set separate landscaping hours — that is a question we put to the office rather than assume an answer to.

Also in the handbook

  • Washing or repairing construction vehicles is prohibited anywhere in the Town
  • Dumpsters fenced or screened, at least ten feet from property lines where practical, emptied within forty-eight hours
  • Loose trash outside the dumpster removed within twenty-four hours
  • Portable toilets screened, not facing the street, not installed before the master permit issues, and removed before the certificate of occupancy
  • Hurricane non-compliance draws a stop-work order and a fine of up to $5,000
  • Off-site damage bonds of $5,000 or $10,000 on certain projects
  • An owner with a valid permit may seek relief from the Town Commission, with mailed notice to owners within three hundred feet at least fourteen days beforehand
  • Penalties escalate to revocation of the Town Occupational Registration and liens

Town of Highland Beach Building Department, 3616 S Ocean Blvd · 561-278-4540 · permits@highlandbeach.us. The same office issues permits for the Town of Gulf Stream under an interlocal agreement, on different hours.

Eight months of darkness

Ordinance 554 and the turtles

From the first of March to the thirty-first of October, Highland Beach requires all beachfront lighting off from dusk to dawn. That is eight months of the year, and it reaches landscape and architectural lighting on the ocean side of every property in the town. It is also the reason a lighting scheme here has to be designed once, correctly, rather than adjusted each spring — and the reason we survey the ocean elevation’s fittings as a distinct item rather than lumping them in with the rest of the house.

Questions

What Highland Beach owners and boards ask first

What are the construction hours in Highland Beach?

Under Chapter 12 of the Town Code, work is prohibited on weekdays before 8:00am or after 6:00pm, Saturdays are limited to 9:00am until 3:00pm, all construction is prohibited on Sundays, and work is prohibited on New Year’s Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas. Workers may not arrive more than thirty minutes before permitted hours or leave more than thirty minutes after. The Town sets and revises these, so we confirm them with the Building Department each season.

Can a dumpster be delivered or emptied on a Saturday?

No. Saturday is a restricted day even inside its nine-to-three window. Section 12-6(b) specifically prohibits placing, cleaning or removing dumpsters and portable restrooms, concrete delivery and pumping, dump trucks, cranes and bulldozers, and jackhammering. What remains permissible is quiet work that causes no noise disturbance across the property line.

Is there a decibel limit in Highland Beach?

No, and that makes it stricter rather than looser. Section 12-7 sets no numeric limit; the test is whether noise annoys, disturbs, injures or endangers the comfort, repose, health, peace or safety of a considerable number of persons. There is no meter reading to point to in your defence. The same section also prohibits contractors, employees and agents from playing music at a site at any time.

What happens to our condominium or house while we are away?

That is most of the year in this town — over forty per cent of Highland Beach housing stood vacant at the last census and three in five residents are over sixty-five. Under a programme the calendar does not pause: dehumidification is held rather than switched off, the property is walked and photographed from fixed positions, water is run, mechanical plant is serviced on its interval, and the place is opened properly before anyone arrives rather than discovered on the day.

Can you work with our association rather than individual unit owners?

Yes, and for most of this town that is the sensible arrangement. Shared plant, common-area glass, walkways, railings, garages and pool decks are one property with one exposure, and splitting them across several vendors is where the failures happen — at the seams between contracts. Amalfi Mechanical holds the plant on a quarterly agreement and Amalfi Estates holds the envelope, on one statement.

Why is our ocean-side lighting switched off for most of the year?

Ordinance 554 requires all beachfront lighting off from dusk to dawn between the first of March and the thirty-first of October — eight months. It reaches landscape and architectural lighting on the ocean side of the property, not just floodlights. We treat the ocean elevation’s fittings as a separate survey item for this reason, and a lighting scheme here is best designed once to comply rather than adjusted each spring.

Do you charge for the survey?

No. Every engagement begins with a survey at no cost and the written report is yours whether or not you engage us. For a residence that is a walk of the exterior, the systems and any waterfront structures. For an association it is an equipment inventory — make, model, serial, age, tonnage, refrigerant and condition of every unit — with a repair-or-replace list in priority order.
