Estates · Programme VII

The wall decides, and the rule decides the wall

Every waterfront owner on this coast is told their seawall will need work one day. Almost none of them are told that Broward County has written down how high it must then be, and that a single repair crossing a defined threshold converts a patch into a rebuild of the whole shoreline. That threshold is the most consequential number on a waterfront parcel, and it is the reason a documented maintenance programme is an argument about money rather than tidiness.

Have the wall walked What a buyer should check

The standard

Five feet, and the dates attached to it

Broward County adopted a regional standard for tidal flood barriers. It is published, it is dated, and almost no owner has read it.

Broward County Code of Ordinances, Chapter 39, Article XXV, section 39-407(a) sets a minimum top elevation of five feet NAVD88 for a tidal flood barrier that is new, substantially repaired or substantially rehabilitated. The model ordinance was adopted on 31 March 2020 under Land Use Plan Policy 2.21.7, and section 39-404 states the standard is designed for conditions through 2070. Tidally influenced municipalities were required to adopt consistent elevations by 13 February 2022, so the number an owner meets is the municipal one, arrived at from the county's.

There is a transition, and it is the part worth knowing now. An application submitted before 1 January 2035 may be permitted at four feet NAVD88, provided the barrier is designed so that it can be raised to five feet by 1 January 2050. That is not a loophole; it is a design obligation that follows the wall. A four-foot wall permitted under the transition has to have been built capable of taking another foot, and an owner buying a property with a recently permitted wall should establish which of the two it is.

The standard does not reach oceanfront beaches or anything seaward of the Coastal Construction Control Line, which are governed separately. It applies to the tidal barriers along the canals and the Intracoastal where most of these houses sit.

The City of Fort Lauderdale set its own minimum top of wall at 5.00 feet NAVD88 under Ordinance No. C23-05, adopted 23 March 2023. We have not been able to verify from the published master plan what compliance deadline that ordinance imposes on private owners, and we are not going to print a date we cannot source. It is the first question to put to the city in writing, and the answer should go in the property file.

What to establish about your wall

  • Its surveyed top elevation, in NAVD88
  • Which municipality's standard applies, and its figure
  • Whether it was permitted under the four-foot transition
  • If so, whether it was designed to be raised to five feet
  • The permit history, and whether each permit was closed
  • Whether a prior repair crossed the substantial threshold
  • Whether it is coral rock, which is treated differently
  • The date and author of the last condition assessment

The threshold

The sentence that turns a repair into a rebuild

This is the single most financially consequential fact about a seawall on this coast, and it is published nowhere in the service market.

The five-foot standard is triggered by three things: construction of a new barrier; installation of fixed infrastructure attached to the barrier; and substantial repair or rehabilitation. The ordinance defines that third one. It means modifying more than fifty per cent of the property's shoreline length, or modifying an appurtenant structure such as a mooring structure at a cost exceeding fifty per cent of the barrier's cost.

And then the part that decides the budget: where the work is substantial, it must extend along the entire shoreline. Not the part that failed. The whole of it, to the standard.

Read that against how seawall work actually gets commissioned. An owner notices spalling along one stretch, waits, notices more, waits again, and eventually commissions a single large repair covering the damaged sixty per cent of the frontage. That project is substantial. It therefore has to be designed, permitted and built to the elevation standard along one hundred per cent of the shoreline, including the forty per cent that was perfectly sound. The wall the owner thought they were buying and the wall they are now required to build are different objects with different prices.

The same frontage addressed as a programme of smaller, separately documented repairs, each kept clearly below the threshold and each recorded, is a different financial event entirely. We are not describing a way around the ordinance, and splitting a single contemplated project to evade the threshold is exactly the thing a reviewer looks for. We are describing the ordinary consequence of maintaining a wall continuously rather than deferring it: deterioration addressed at the surface, in the year it appears, does not accumulate into a fifty-per-cent event.

That is the whole argument for the calendar. Not that photographs are nice to have, but that a wall under observation stays on one side of a line in the county code, and a wall left alone crosses it.

What counts as substantial

  • More than 50% of the property's shoreline length modified
  • An appurtenant structure modified at over 50% of the barrier's cost
  • Substantial work must then extend along the entire shoreline
  • Splitting one project to stay under the line is not available
  • A new barrier, or fixed infrastructure attached to one, also triggers it

The clock

What the county can require, and how fast

The obligations run with ownership, the presumption runs against a leaking wall, and the deadlines are short.

Keep it in good repair

An owner obligation, not a recommendation. The county frames the duty as continuing, which is why a dated record of condition over time is worth holding.

Through-flow is presumed disrepair

A barrier that lets tidal water pass unimpeded is presumed to be in disrepair, and that is a citable offence. Water coming through is not a cosmetic failure; it is the evidential threshold.

Trespass is a public nuisance

A barrier below the standard that allows tidal water to reach adjacent property or the public right-of-way is declared a public nuisance, and that too is citable. Your wall's shortfall becomes your neighbour's grievance with legal shape.

Sixty days, then three hundred and sixty-five

On citation the owner must show progress within sixty days and complete within three hundred and sixty-five. If the repair meets the substantial threshold, the whole sequence — design, permit, build, final inspection, on a compliant wall — has to fit inside that year.

How it has to be built

Construction must stop through-flow while still relieving hydrostatic pressure from the upland side, connect to the adjoining barriers where practicable, and carry natural limerock rip-rap or approved habitat enhancement on the waterward face.

And it follows you to closing

Under section 39-408, sales contracts executed after 31 December 2020 must carry a specified disclosure. A documented maintenance history is the difference between a disclosure that reassures a buyer and one that reopens the price.

The calendar

How often a wall is actually looked at

The City of Fort Lauderdale's own seawall master plan sets inspection intervals by the age of the wall, and the shape of them surprises people.

Wall ageInspection interval
0–5 yearsEvery 6 months
6–10 yearsAnnually
11–15 yearsEvery 2 years
16–20 yearsEvery 3 years
20+ yearsEvery 4–5 years

Owners assume the opposite — that an old wall needs watching and a new one can be left. The interval runs the other way because the early years are when a construction or installation defect declares itself, and because a fault found in year three is a warranty conversation while the same fault found in year nine is an owner's bill. Two cautions on the source: the 2024 master plan is a consultant's report prepared on the City's own walls, and it contains an internal inconsistency about which walls are excluded from raising. We use the intervals as a sound professional baseline for a private programme, not as a regulation imposed on private owners. Coral rock walls are excluded from raising and are repaired or replaced instead.

What we look at

Four markers, in the order they matter

The City's plan ranks severity on four conditions. They are what a documented inspection should photograph and grade, and the last two are the ones that end a wall.

  1. CrackingMapped, measured and photographed from fixed positions so the same crack is compared with itself across years rather than across memories. Pattern matters more than width: a crack running parallel to the cap behaves differently from one running down a panel joint.
  2. SpallingConcrete breaking away from the face. It is the visible consequence of what is happening inside, and by the time a section has spalled the mechanism has been running for some time.
  3. Corrosive stainingRust bleed at the surface, and the early tell that chloride has reached the reinforcement. This is the one owners dismiss as a stain. It is not a stain; it is the structure reporting on itself, and the same mechanism splits balconies and lintels — we have written on how to read it in a wall, and the physics is identical at the water.
  4. Exposed reinforcementSteel visible at the surface. The late stage, and the point at which the question stops being whether to repair and becomes how much of the frontage is involved — which is the threshold question in the section above.
  5. The land side, every timeDepressions, sinking pavers, a terrace or pool deck settling in a line parallel to the water. Soil escaping through or under a wall shows in the garden before it shows on the wall, and it is the cheapest early warning available.
  6. Weeps and drainageA wall holds back water as well as soil, and pressure behind it has to relieve somewhere. Blocked or absent weeps raise hydrostatic pressure and accelerate everything above. Cheap to check, cheap to clear, expensive to ignore.

The dock

Hardware, anodes and the things that fail first

The dock is the part owners look at and the part that is easiest to keep, provided the right metal was specified and the inspection goes below the waterline.

Chloride and marine growth are the two enemies, and the first of them is specific. Stainless steel resists corrosion because of a passive oxide film, and chloride is the ion that breaks that film down and initiates pitting and crevice corrosion. Grade 316 contains molybdenum and resists it materially better than grade 304, which looks identical on the shelf, costs less, and will bleed rust within a season here. It is the most common and most avoidable specification failure on the water, and it applies to every fastener, bracket, cleat and through-bolt, not just the visible hardware.

Salt deposits make it worse in a way that is easy to miss. They are hygroscopic: they draw moisture out of humid air and hold a conductive film on the metal long after the rain has stopped, so corrosion carries on in dry weather. That is why the fresh-water rinse after a strong onshore blow is scheduled as maintenance rather than offered as cleaning, and why it is not reserved for named storms.

Below the waterline is where the inspection earns its fee. Piling and substructure fail in the splash and tidal zones, which are the parts nobody looks at from the deck. Zinc anodes on a boat lift have a service life and a visible condition, and an anode that has done its job has consumed itself — a pristine anode after two years is not good news, it means the circuit is not working and the steel is being consumed instead.

What we do here is bounded, and we state it plainly below. Cleaning, inspection and recording, and like-for-like hardware maintenance. Anything structural belongs to a licensed marine contractor and to a permit.

On the dock cycle

  • Hardware checked and photographed from fixed positions
  • Fasteners and through-bolts specified in grade 316, never 304
  • Cleats, bumpers and ladder rungs replaced like for like
  • Lift hardware lubricated, cable and sheave condition recorded
  • Zinc anode condition read and photographed, not assumed
  • Piling and substructure inspected below the waterline
  • Fresh-water rinse after any strong onshore wind
  • A dated photographic record, so change is visible over years

The boundary

What we do, and who does the rest

Stated plainly, because on the water the line between maintenance and licensed construction is where owners get into trouble.

A licensed marine contractor, under permit

  • Panel repair, cap replacement, tieback and pile work
  • Raising or rebuilding a wall to the elevation standard
  • Any work meeting the substantial repair threshold
  • Dock, boathouse, pier and boat ramp construction or alteration
  • Bulkhead work and marine pile driving
  • Anything requiring Broward environmental review, FDEP approval,
  • or, for work in navigable waters, the US Army Corps of Engineers

Marine work is a licensed specialty. The Marine Specialty Contractor category under Board Rule 61G4-15.033 covers construction, repair and alteration of docks, boathouses, seawalls, bulkheads, piers and boat ramps, and Broward operates narrower local categories for seawall, bulkhead, dock and pile driving work. That contract is one an owner holds directly with the marine contractor, which is the right way round: the party carrying the structural risk is the party answerable for it, and nobody sits in between taking a margin on the work. What we hold is the record, the calendar and the condition grading — which is what tells an owner which conversation is coming, and roughly when.

Questions

What owners ask

How high does my seawall have to be in Broward County?

Broward County Code section 39-407(a) sets a minimum top elevation of five feet NAVD88 for tidal flood barriers that are new, substantially repaired or substantially rehabilitated, under a model ordinance adopted 31 March 2020 and designed for conditions through 2070. Tidally influenced municipalities had to adopt consistent elevations by 13 February 2022, so the figure that governs your parcel is the municipal one derived from the county standard. An application submitted before 1 January 2035 may be permitted at four feet, provided the barrier is designed so that it can be raised to five feet by 1 January 2050. The standard does not reach oceanfront beaches or anything seaward of the Coastal Construction Control Line.

What does “substantial repair” mean for a seawall?

It is defined, and it is the most expensive definition on a waterfront parcel. Substantial repair or rehabilitation means modifying more than fifty per cent of the property's shoreline length, or modifying an appurtenant structure such as a mooring structure at a cost exceeding fifty per cent of the barrier's cost. Where work is substantial it must extend along the entire shoreline, to the elevation standard — not just the part that failed. A deferred repair that eventually covers sixty per cent of the frontage therefore obliges you to rebuild all of it.

Can I just keep patching the wall instead?

Continuous maintenance is exactly what keeps a wall on the right side of that threshold, and that is a legitimate and sensible way to own a seawall. What is not available is splitting a single project you have already decided to do into pieces in order to stay under fifty per cent — a reviewer looks at the substance. The distinction is between deterioration addressed in the year it appears, which never accumulates into a substantial event, and a large project dressed up as several small ones.

What happens if the county cites my seawall?

You must show progress within sixty days and complete the work within three hundred and sixty-five. If the repair meets the substantial threshold, design, permitting, construction and final inspection on a compliant wall all have to fit inside that year. Two things create the citation: a barrier that lets tidal water pass unimpeded is presumed to be in disrepair, and a barrier below the standard that allows tidal water to reach adjacent property or the public right-of-way is declared a public nuisance.

How often should a seawall be inspected?

The City of Fort Lauderdale's seawall master plan uses intervals by wall age: every six months for the first five years, annually from six to ten, every two years from eleven to fifteen, every three years from sixteen to twenty, and every four to five years beyond that. New walls are watched more closely than old ones, which is the reverse of what owners expect — the early years are when a construction defect declares itself, and a fault found in year three is a warranty conversation rather than a bill. Those intervals come from a consultant's report on City-owned walls; we use them as a professional baseline for private programmes, not as a rule imposed on private owners.

Does the seawall have to be disclosed when I sell?

Under section 39-408, sales contracts executed after 31 December 2020 must include a specified disclosure. Which is a practical reason to hold a dated record: a disclosure accompanied by years of photographs, condition grades and completed repairs is a very different document from a disclosure accompanied by nothing, and the difference shows up in the price.

What stainless should dock hardware be?

Grade 316. It contains molybdenum and resists chloride, which is the ion that breaks down the passive film on stainless and starts pitting. Grade 304 looks identical, costs less and will bleed rust within a season in this environment. It applies to every fastener, bracket, cleat and through-bolt. Salt deposits compound it because they are hygroscopic — they pull moisture from humid air and keep a conductive film on the metal long after the rain stops, which is why the fresh-water rinse after a strong onshore wind is scheduled rather than occasional.

Who repairs the wall itself?

A licensed marine contractor, under permit, holding the contract directly with you. Marine construction, repair and alteration is a licensed specialty under Board Rule 61G4-15.033, and Broward operates narrower local categories for seawall, bulkhead, dock and pile driving work. We do the cleaning, the inspection, the photographic record, the condition grading, the hardware maintenance and the sealant and weep work — and we tell you which conversation is coming and roughly when, so the repair is commissioned on your timetable rather than after a citation.
