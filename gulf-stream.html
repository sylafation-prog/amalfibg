Town of Gulf Stream · Four hours, day or night

Estate Care in Gulf Stream

Gulf Stream is the most tightly regulated town we work in, and it is not close. Mowing and pruning here are not landscaping in the eyes of the Town — they are regulated construction activity, on the same clock as a jackhammer. From the first of December to the first of May that clock allows weekdays only. A vendor who does not know that will be stopped, fined, and eventually struck off the Town register.

In town

What we hold in Gulf Stream

Amalfi Estates keeps private residences in Gulf Stream to a written calendar of care, under one named steward and a single monthly statement. Dispatch runs at every hour, with a four-hour emergency target in Palm Beach County, and every engagement begins with a survey at no cost.

The Core district

The heart of the town, where the Neighborhood Character Preservation Overlay exists expressly to hold the low, horizontal character — incentivising single-storey houses, capping second-floor area at a fraction of the first and roof height at twenty-eight feet.

North Ocean Boulevard

Its own overlay district, running between the Atlantic and the Intracoastal, and the stretch along which the Town designates its construction staging. The most exposed elevations in the town face this road.

Place Au Soleil

The mainland-side neighbourhood, treated separately in the construction rules — the A1A staging designation expressly does not apply here, which changes how work is planned on this side of the water.

Golf and club frontage

Houses backing the Gulf Stream Golf Club and the waterway, where the grounds are as much of the property as the building and the standard expected of them is correspondingly higher.

The rule that governs everything

Here, mowing is construction

Section 22-67(3) of the Town Code defines “regulated activities” to include planting, pruning, mowing and grinding. There is no separate, gentler schedule for landscape maintenance. Your gardener and a demolition crew are on the same timetable, and that timetable tightens for five months of the year.

PeriodWhat is permitted
1 May – 1 Dec Regulated activities on non-holiday weekdays and Saturdays, 8:00am to 5:00pm. No Sunday work.
1 Dec – 1 May Weekdays 8:00am to 5:00pm only. Saturday work is permitted solely inside a fully enclosed structure, with no detectable noise, dust, odour, vibration or bright light at any other property. No Sunday work.
1 Dec – 1 May Restricted activities prohibited outright: pile drivers, dredges, dry sandblasting, jackhammers, and concrete mixing and pumping equipment. Concrete trucks and hand-turned mixers are excluded from the prohibition.
Both periods Arrival in town to perform regulated activities no earlier than 8:00am, departure by 5:00pm. There is no early staging on the street.

Read that last row again, because it is the one most firms fall foul of. The ordinance does not say work begins at eight. It says arrival in town is no earlier than eight. A crew that pulls up at half past seven to unload and have a coffee is already in breach before anybody has touched a tool, and the Commission said so in plain words when it adopted the provision: there can be no arrival of workmen before eight o’clock, and they must all depart by five.

The practical consequence for an owner is a shorter working year. Between December and May the loud exterior work simply cannot be done, and the Saturday overflow that every other town allows is gone. A property here needs its noisy maintenance planned into the summer and autumn deliberately, a year ahead, or it does not get done at all. That is a scheduling problem rather than a trade problem, and it is the single biggest reason houses in this town fall behind.

Responsibility runs upward. A companion resolution makes the general contractor accountable for subcontractor compliance, so an owner who hires a coordinator rather than a crew is buying someone whose job is to know this — and the Town registers every contractor regardless of licence type, at no fee, with insurance and bond kept current with both the County and the Town, or permits are simply denied.

Hours, dates and the handbook itself are the Town’s to set and the Town’s to change. We confirm them each season with the Building Department rather than working from last year’s copy, and so should anybody else working here.

One portal, two rulebooks

Gulf Stream permits are issued by Highland Beach

The two towns share a permit office. Applications go through Highland Beach’s portal on Highland Beach forms, no paper accepted — but Gulf Stream keeps zoning authority, its zoning sign-off must come first, and the two towns run entirely different construction-hours regimes. Highland Beach allows a Saturday window between nine and three. Gulf Stream, in season, does not allow one at all. Getting those two the wrong way round is an easy mistake and an expensive one.

Questions

What Gulf Stream owners ask first

Can my landscape crew work on a Saturday in Gulf Stream?

Between the first of May and the first of December, yes — non-holiday Saturdays, eight to five. Between the first of December and the first of May, no. The Town Code defines planting, pruning, mowing and grinding as regulated activities on the same schedule as construction, and in that period Saturday work is permitted only inside a fully enclosed structure with no detectable noise, dust, odour, vibration or light reaching any other property. Sunday work is not permitted in either period. This is stricter than any neighbouring town, and it is the single thing most vendors here get wrong.

When can a crew actually arrive on site?

Not before eight in the morning. The ordinance governs arrival in the Town, not the start of work, so unloading or waiting on the street at half past seven is already a breach. Departure is by five. We stage crews to arrive together, work the property through and leave, rather than coming and going across the day.

What cannot be done at all during the season?

From the first of December to the first of May the Town prohibits a defined list of restricted activities outright: pile drivers, dredges, dry sandblasting, jackhammers and concrete mixing and pumping equipment. Concrete trucks and hand-turned mixers are excluded from that prohibition. Practically, it means heavy exterior work on a Gulf Stream property has to be planned into the summer and autumn a season in advance.

Who pulls the permit, Gulf Stream or Highland Beach?

Both, in order. Gulf Stream retains zoning authority and its zoning sign-off comes first; permits and inspections are then issued by the Town of Highland Beach under an interlocal agreement, through Highland Beach’s portal and on Highland Beach forms, with no paper applications accepted. The two towns also run different construction-hours regimes, so the office you are filing with is not the office whose clock you are on.

Does our contractor have to be registered with the Town?

Yes. Every contractor, regardless of licence type, must register with Gulf Stream — there is no fee — and keep insurance and bond current with both Palm Beach County and the Town, or permits are denied. A third violation of the construction rules can revoke that registration, which is a considerably more serious consequence than a fine.

Where do delivery trucks and cranes go?

All vehicles belong entirely on site, or at an alternate approved by the Planning and Building Administrator; right-of-way parking is prohibited and washing or repairing a vehicle anywhere in the Town is prohibited. The designated staging area is the A1A shoulder between 1919 and 2411 North Ocean Boulevard, and it does not apply in Place Au Soleil. Trucks over nine tons loaded and all cranes must use the staging area and wait for a mandatory police escort.

Do you charge for the survey?

No. Every engagement begins with a survey at no cost and the written report is yours whether or not you engage us — a walk of the exterior, the systems and the grounds, photographed and written up with a recommended calendar of care built around the Town’s own working year.
