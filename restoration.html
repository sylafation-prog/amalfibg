Water damage restoration

Water that sits turns a dry-out into a reconstruction. On a coastal house the damage runs behind the cabinetry, under the stone, through the millwork and into the mechanical before anyone sees a mark on the ceiling. We extract, dry and document to the IICRC standard — and because the houses we keep are photographed every month, we can show what yours looked like before the water.

Time

The first hours decide the cost

A burst supply line found at two in the morning is a plumbing bill. The same line found on Monday is a kitchen. Nothing about the water changes in between; what changes is what it has reached and what it has become.

Clean water does not stay clean

The IICRC S500 standard sorts water into three categories by what it carries. Category 1 is a supply line or rain through a window. Left standing in a warm house, in contact with carpet pad, gypsum and whatever is under the floor, it degrades to Category 2 in about two days and to Category 3 after that. Each step up changes what can be dried in place and what has to come out, and the step is a matter of time, not of what broke.

Gypsum and plaster

Drywall wet for a few hours dries in place. Drywall wet for days loses its paper bond, swells at the joints and begins to grow what the humidity feeds. The flood cut that could have been avoided becomes the only honest option, and the paint, the finish and the base all go with it.

Hardwood

Solid and engineered floors take water from the underside first. Cupping shows within days and is usually recoverable if the drying starts promptly and is done from above and below, on mats, against readings. Left to crown and buckle, the floor is finished — and often the subfloor with it.

Cabinetry and millwork

Kitchen and vanity carcases are particleboard and MDF behind the veneer, and they wick from the toe-kick up. The first days decide whether the run is dried in place from behind or replaced, along with the stone that sits on it and the backsplash that is grouted to the wall.

Stone and what is under it

Travertine and marble are porous. Water that reaches the mortar bed and the substrate does not show on the surface until it has been there long enough to stain, lift or bloom white at the joints. A moisture meter finds it in the first hour. Looking finds it in the third week.

Florida humidity

Outside, the air is already near eighty per cent. A wet house with the air conditioning off is an incubator: microbial growth begins within a day or two, and the air begins to carry it through the house. Dehumidification and air movement from the first hours are what keep a water loss from becoming an air-quality problem.

Before anyone arrives

  • Stop the water at the source if you can do it safely — the appliance stop, the supply valve, the main
  • Do not step into standing water where it may have reached an outlet, a cord or the panel
  • Keep the air conditioning running if it is safe to; it is the first dehumidifier
  • Lift what can be lifted off the floor — rugs, skirted furniture, the lower drawers, books
  • Photograph everything before anything is moved
  • Keep what the water ruined; nothing is discarded before it is recorded

The difference

We can show what the house looked like before the water

Most of what is argued about after a water loss is not the water. It is whether the damage was there before. On a house we already keep, that question has an answer, with a date on it.

Every house on the estate calendar is walked and photographed on every visit: the exterior condition walk monthly, the interior on house watch, the glass, the stone, the terraces, the mechanical rooms, and the cabinets under the sinks where the supply lines live. The photographs are dated, taken the same way each time, and kept by us. Nobody has to think about them, and nobody does — until the day there is water on the floor.

Carriers routinely dispute whether damage pre-existed the event. An adjuster who sees cupped hardwood, a stained ceiling or a swollen vanity three days after a loss has no way of knowing whether it was cupped, stained or swollen three months before — and a house with a dozen other things going on can be read either way. It is the most common reason a large claim is reduced, and in most cases the owner has nothing to answer it with except their own word and whatever happens to be on their telephone.

Our clients have something else: a dated, systematic, third-party record of the same room, the same floor and the same run of cabinets in good condition, taken by a firm that had no idea there would ever be a claim. Alongside the readings and the photographs we take on the day, that record helps substantiate what the water did and what it did not. It does not decide a claim — nothing on our side of the work can, and we do not advise on coverage — but it means the question of what was there before is not left to memory.

No restoration company can produce this. Not because it is difficult, but because it requires having been in the house every month before anything went wrong. That is what the estate calendar is, and this page is one of the reasons it exists.

The standard

How a dry-out runs

The work follows the IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration, the reference the trade and the carriers both recognise. The sequence below is what that means in a house, in the order it happens.

  1. First call and triageWhat happened, where the water came from, whether it has stopped and what it has reached. What to shut off. Whether a licensed plumber, roofer or electrician is needed for the source. What is arranged, and you are told what it is.
  2. Safety, category and classOn arrival: the power, the structure and the water itself. The water is categorised by its source and what it has touched — clean supply, grey or black — and the loss is classed by how much wet material there is and how hard it will be to evaporate. Those two determinations set the plan and decide what can be saved.
  3. Source controlNothing dries while water is still arriving. The supply is isolated, the roof opening covered, the appliance disconnected. Where the source is a plumbing, roofing or electrical repair, a licensed trade makes it, contracting directly with you, and the drying proceeds around them.
  4. ExtractionStanding water is pumped out and the remainder is extracted from carpet, pad and hard surfaces with truck-mounted or portable equipment. Every gallon removed mechanically is a gallon that does not have to be evaporated. Extraction is the fastest drying there is.
  5. Drying plan and equipment placementMoisture readings and a moisture map of every affected material. Drying goals set against a dry standard taken from unaffected material of the same kind in the same house. Air movers, dehumidifiers and specialty drying — floor mats, wall-cavity injection — placed and sized to the class of the loss, not to the size of the truck.
  6. Daily monitoring against the goalsEvery day, every affected material is metered and the reading recorded against its drying goal. Temperature, relative humidity and grains of moisture are logged in each room, outside, and at the dehumidifier. Equipment is moved, added or removed on the numbers. The day's readings are in your hands that day.
  7. Completion against the dry standardThe job is finished when the materials read at the dry standard, not when the equipment has been there a set number of days. The final readings are taken and recorded, and the equipment comes out.
  8. The documentation packageThe moisture logs, the psychrometric readings, the equipment log, the moisture maps, the photographs from first walk to final reading, and an itemised estimate of our own work, in one file: to you, and at your direction, to your adjuster.

The boundary

Where we stop, and who takes over

Three lines are drawn by Florida statute, and we hold to them. Much of the trade blurs them. Being plain about where they fall is how you know the rest of what we say is true.

Mold

Florida licenses mold assessment and mold remediation as separate trades, and growth beyond ten square feet belongs to them. If we find or expose growth past that threshold we stop, contain it, photograph it, record where it is and how much, and tell you the same day. You engage a licensed mold remediator directly, and the law keeps that firm separate from the one that assesses: the same company cannot both assess and remediate the same property within twelve months. We keep the drying and the containment going around their work and hand them our readings.

Reconstruction

Drying ends with the materials at the dry standard and the flood cuts open. Putting the house back — drywall, floors, cabinetry, stone, paint — is contracting under Chapter 489, and it is performed by a licensed contractor who contracts directly with you. Amalfi Construction can take it on, directing licensed trades that contract with you as on every project; if you prefer your own contractor, the drying file goes to them.

Plumbing and electrical

The failed supply line, the appliance valve, the roof that let the water in, the panel that got wet: each is a licensed trade. We isolate the source so the drying can begin, and the licensed plumber, roofer or electrician makes the repair under a contract with you. We work around them and record what they found.

The file

What we give your adjuster

We are not your adjuster and we do not act as one. We do not file, negotiate or advise on your claim, and we do not interpret your policy — that is for your adjuster, your broker or a licensed public adjuster. What we do is document the loss better than anyone else in the house, and hand it over.

A water claim is decided on paper. The adjuster who was not there on the first night has to reconstruct what happened from what the restorer wrote down, and a restorer who kept poor notes costs the owner money without ever meaning to. So the file is treated as part of the work, and it is built from the first call rather than assembled afterwards.

Everything we record is about our own work and what we observed: where the water was, what it reached, what it read, what we did about it, and what it read when we finished. Our estimate itemises our scope and our pricing and nothing else. We will explain any line of it to you or to your adjuster. We will not tell either of you what the policy covers, because we do not know and are not permitted to say.

Where the house is on the estate calendar, the file begins before the loss, with the condition record described above. Where it is not, the file begins the moment the first photograph is taken on arrival, and it is the most complete account of the loss anyone will have.

Questions

Before you call

Should I call my insurer before I call you?

Stop the water first, then call whoever you need to. Your policy sets out what notice your carrier requires and when; that is a question for your agent or your adjuster, and we do not advise on it. What we can tell you is that drying does not wait well. The clock on secondary damage starts the moment the water does, and the documentation we build from the first visit is the same whether the claim is opened that day or the next.

Who pays you?

You do. You engage us in writing, our estimate itemises our own work line by line, and we invoice you for it. Whether and how much your carrier reimburses you is between you and your carrier. We do not file, negotiate or manage claims, and nothing we ask you to sign gives us any claim against your insurer.

What is an assignment of benefits, and why does it no longer apply?

An assignment of benefits was a document a restoration company asked you to sign on the first night, transferring your rights under the policy to the company so it could bill and pursue your carrier directly. Florida law now makes a post-loss assignment of benefits void on residential and commercial property policies issued on or after 1 January 2023, so the arrangement no longer exists for anyone. You contract with us and we invoice you, your claim stays yours, and nobody between you and your carrier holds a piece of it.

What happens if you find mold?

We stop, contain it, photograph it, and record where it is and how much. Florida licenses mold assessment and mold remediation as separate trades, and growth beyond ten square feet belongs to them. You engage a licensed mold remediator directly, and the law requires that the firm which assesses the mold and the firm which removes it be different companies for twelve months, so you are not at the mercy of one opinion. We keep the drying and the containment going around their work and hand them our readings.

How long does drying take?

It depends on what is wet, how wet, and for how long before we started. A clean-water loss caught the same day on tile and drywall is a different job from hardwood and cabinetry that sat over a weekend. The honest answer is that the job is finished when the materials read at the dry standard, and not before. The readings are taken daily and you see them daily, so you always know where it stands and why the equipment is still there.

Can you do the repairs afterwards?

The drying ends with the materials dry and the flood cuts open. Rebuilding — drywall, floors, cabinetry, stone, paint — is construction, and in Florida it is performed by a licensed contractor who contracts directly with you. Amalfi Construction can take it on, directing licensed trades that contract with you as on every project; if you prefer your own contractor, the drying file goes to them. Either way the drying contract and the construction contract are separate, and you are never paying one firm for the other's work.

What do you give my adjuster?

The same file we give you, at your direction: daily moisture logs by material and location, psychrometric readings, the equipment log, moisture maps, photographs from arrival to completion, the source and cause as we observed it, and an itemised estimate of our own work. Where the house is on the estate calendar, the dated condition record from before the loss goes with it. We will explain any line of our estimate to your adjuster. We do not interpret your policy or advise on coverage; that is for your adjuster, your broker or a licensed public adjuster.

Do you need a licence to do this work in Florida?

No. Water extraction, structural drying, dehumidification, air scrubbing, moisture monitoring, antimicrobial application and the removal of wet carpet, pad and drywall as part of drying are not a licensed trade under Florida's contracting statute, and a Florida appellate court confirmed in 2024 that dry-out contracts do not require a contractor's licence. The lines are drawn around that scope: mold above ten square feet, reconstruction, plumbing and electrical repair each require a licence, and each is performed by a licensed trade that contracts directly with you. Certificates of insurance are in your hands before anyone arrives.
