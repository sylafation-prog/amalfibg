Condominiums · Reserve components

Not maintenance. A statutory reserve line.

Of the seven components Florida names in the structural integrity reserve study, one is not structure, not plant and not a system: waterproofing and exterior painting. Boards that had treated repainting as discretionary upkeep — deferrable in a tight year, waivable by a vote — now find it in the same schedule as the roof and the load-bearing walls. This page explains why, and what follows.

The statute

Where it sits, and what that changes

The list is short and the company it keeps is the point.

Named reserve componentWhat boards historically did with it
RoofReserved for it
Structure, including load-bearing walls and primary structural members and systemsReserved for it
Fireproofing and fire protection systemsReserved for it
PlumbingReserved for it
Electrical systemsReserved for it
Waterproofing and exterior paintingTreated as operating maintenance. Deferred in tight years. Waived by vote.
Windows and exterior doorsSometimes reserved, often not
Any other item with deferred maintenance or replacement cost above $25,000 whose failure would negatively affect the aboveVaries

Three consequences follow from that row being in that list. First, the reserve study must identify the component’s remaining useful life and replacement cost and set a funding schedule that reaches that cost by the end of that life. Second, for budgets adopted on or after 31 December 2024, members of a unit-owner-controlled association required to obtain a study may not vote to waive or reduce reserves for it. Third, funds reserved for it may not be used for anything else.

The second of those is the change boards feel. For decades the annual waiver vote was a routine item at the budget meeting, and exterior painting was among the first things it reached. That option has gone for most associations, with a narrow exception for multicondominium associations operating a Division-approved alternative funding method.

There is one temporary flexibility and it is worth knowing precisely. Through 31 December 2028, an association that completed a milestone inspection within the previous two calendar years may vote — by a majority of total voting interests — to pause or reduce reserve contributions for up to two consecutive annual budgets in order to pay for the repairs that inspection identified. After the pause, a reserve study must be performed before contributions resume. A deferral with a condition, not a waiver, and it ends.

The other provision worth knowing: where reserves are pooled, a pool covering the statutory components may contain only other statutory components. An association cannot pool exterior painting with the clubhouse furniture and draw on it for either.

Why the Legislature put it there is not stated in the statute, but it is not mysterious. In a marine climate the coating and sealant system is what keeps water out of the structure, and water in the structure is what corrodes reinforcement and spalls concrete. Exterior painting is not cosmetic on a coastal building. It is the outermost layer of structural protection, and the item immediately above it in the list is the one it protects.

Interval

How often it is actually due here

The number a reserve study uses for remaining useful life is frequently inherited from a generic schedule, and generic schedules are written for a continental climate.

A well-specified elastomeric or high-build acrylic system on sound stucco, properly prepared, will commonly give eight to twelve years of service on a sheltered inland elevation in South Florida. On a direct-exposure coastal elevation — salt aerosol, driving rain, high ultraviolet, afternoon sun — five to eight years is a more honest expectation, and the oceanfront south and west faces of a building can reach the end of their service life while the north face still looks new.

Sealant is shorter. A high-performance silicone or polyurethane joint to ASTM C920 that was correctly designed and installed may give fifteen to twenty years. One that was topped over an undersized joint, installed without backer rod, or allowed three-sided adhesion will fail in three to five, and a great many of the joints on a thirty-year-old building are in that second category.

The practical consequence is that a single building-wide interval in a reserve study is usually wrong in both directions at once. It over-reserves for the protected elevations and under-reserves for the exposed ones, and because the exposed ones fail first, the association discovers the shortfall exactly when it needs the money.

The better input to a study, and the one we produce, is a condition assessment by elevation with a remaining service life for each. It is more work to generate once. It then makes the next three budget cycles considerably more predictable and it makes phasing possible, which is the single largest cost lever a board has on envelope work.

A note for anyone having a study prepared now. Fla. Stat. §718.112 permits a study to recommend no reserves for an item with a remaining useful life over twenty-five years, but requires that such items be separately identified. Exterior coating will never be one of them in this climate, and a study treating it as a long-life item should be questioned.

Cost

What drives the number

Boards want a dollar per square foot. The spread between a well-prepared building and a badly prepared one is wider than the spread between contractors, which is why the figure is less useful than the drivers.

Substrate condition

The largest variable by a wide margin. Coating a sound, previously well-coated wall is a predictable operation. Coating a wall with delaminated stucco, failed previous elastomeric, hollow areas behind the render and twenty years of patches in four different mixes is substrate repair with a coating at the end of it, and the repair is most of the cost.

Access

Swing stage, mast climber, boom lift, pipe scaffold or rope access, and how many drops the building geometry forces. A simple rectangular tower is cheap to access. Courtyards, return walls, setbacks, balcony overhangs and a pool deck that cannot take a lift are each a cost. On a tall slender building access can approach the cost of the work.

Sealant quantity

Linear feet of perimeter, control, expansion and penetration joints. On a building with a lot of glazing this is a large number, and whether existing joints must be cut out to a correct dimension or can be renewed in place changes the labour substantially.

System specification

Two coats of acrylic at 8 mils dry and three coats of high-build elastomeric at 20 mils are different products, different labour and different service lives. Comparing a price without comparing the system is comparing nothing.

Occupied-building logistics

Balcony clearing, owner notification, restricted hours, protection of glass, railings, decks and landscaping, and working around a building full of residents who did not ask for this. Real cost, routinely unpriced, and the commonest source of mid-contract friction.

Phasing

Mobilising once for a whole building is cheaper per square foot than mobilising four times for four elevations. Phasing buys cash-flow relief and pays for it in mobilisation and access. Worth doing deliberately where the finances require it; worth not doing where they do not.

We do not publish rates, for a reason that is in the board’s interest rather than ours: a rate quoted without the substrate condition is a guess, and a board that has anchored on a guess will read every subsequent real price as an overcharge. Public data has the same problem at larger scale — OPPAGA’s July 2026 report recorded milestone repair permit values from under $1,000 to $30 million, and the averages across that range are not a budget for anything.

What we do instead is survey the building, quantify it by elevation, write the specification, and produce a cost that a board can tender competitively against other contractors on the same quantities. That is a number that means something, and it is the only kind worth putting in front of owners.

For reserve study purposes specifically, what the study needs is a replacement cost and a remaining useful life per elevation. That is a smaller piece of work than a tender package and we are happy to produce it on its own for a board or a reserve specialist who needs a credible input rather than a generic schedule figure.

One figure boards should know in the funding conversation: the statutory reserve threshold is $25,000, or the inflation-adjusted equivalent where greater. An item with a deferred maintenance or replacement cost above that level whose failure would negatively affect any of the named components must itself be covered by the study. On most buildings exterior coating and sealant clear that threshold by an order of magnitude, which is part of why the component cannot be treated as an operating line.

And a point about how the money may be raised, since it shapes what is realistic. Reserves for the statutory components may be funded by regular assessments, special assessments, lines of credit or loans, with the latter three each requiring a majority of the total voting interests. A board that intends to fund a coating programme by special assessment should begin the notice and meeting process alongside the quantification rather than after the prices arrive.

Specification

What a durable coating programme actually involves

Ten stages. The ones that get cut are stages two, three and nine, and they are the ones that decide whether the system lasts five years or twelve.

  1. Survey and substrate assessment by elevationSounding for delamination and hollow areas, identifying the existing coating and whether it is compatible or must come off, recording crack patterns and their likely cause, and mapping condition by elevation. Everything downstream is priced against this, so a programme that starts without it is priced against assumptions.
  2. Moisture testing before anything elseSubstrate moisture content governs whether a coating will adhere or blister, and on a coastal building with a history of water ingress it is frequently above the manufacturer's limit in exactly the areas that most need coating. Testing costs very little. Coating over trapped moisture costs the entire job.
  3. Removal of failed existing coatingWhere a previous elastomeric has lost adhesion, coating over it transfers the new system's service life to the old system's bond, which is nil. Removal is unwelcome, expensive and sometimes unavoidable, and a bid that does not mention it on a building with failed coating has either not looked or is not saying.
  4. Substrate repair, matched and curedPatching to a compatible mix, built in correct lifts, cured properly, and textured to match. A patch that has not cured will show through the coating as a different sheen and a different colour within a year. A patch in the wrong mix will crack at its perimeter.
  5. Crack treatment appropriate to causeHairline shrinkage cracking, map cracking, movement cracking at a joint that was never formed, and cracking that signals structural movement are four different findings with four different treatments. Filling the fourth one is concealment, not repair, and is the point at which an engineer should be involved rather than a coating contractor.
  6. Joint renewal to a designed dimensionCut out to achieve a width-to-depth ratio around 2:1, backer rod correctly sized to prevent three-sided adhesion, primed where the substrate requires it, sealant to a stated ASTM C920 class and grade tooled into the joint faces. This is the single most commonly abbreviated operation on a facade and the single most common early failure.
  7. Priming with a dedicated primerNot the first coat thinned. A dedicated primer-sealer on a porous or repaired substrate is what delivers uniform absorption and therefore uniform colour and sheen, and it is what the manufacturer's system warranty generally requires.
  8. Coating applied to specified film thicknessWet and dry film thickness measured and recorded, not estimated by coverage rate. Coverage rate assumes a flat wall; a textured stucco elevation consumes materially more, and a crew working to coverage rate on textured stucco will deliver a system thinner than specified.
  9. Inspection under raking light, elevation by elevationLow-angle light reveals texture mismatch, patch shadows, roller lap marks and missed areas that are invisible under direct light. Inspecting before the access comes down is the difference between a punch list and a remobilisation, and it is the stage most often skipped because the building looks finished.
  10. Documentation for the fileProducts and batches, film thickness records, manufacturer inspection reports where a system warranty depends on them, photographs by elevation and date, and the warranty documents themselves. This is what the next reserve study is built from and what a lender or insurer will ask for.

Licensing

What we can take, and on whose authority

Said plainly, because a board is entitled to know before it engages anyone.

Painting, waterproofing, caulking, pressure washing, plastering, stuccoing and decorative stone, tile, marble, granite and terrazzo installation are among the job scopes the Florida Legislature placed outside state contractor licensing under Fla. Stat. §489.117(4)(a). The same provision bars a local government from requiring a state or local licence to obtain a permit for those job scopes. That is the authority we work under and it is a statute, not an interpretation.

What it does not cover: structural repair, roofing, window and door replacement, electrical, plumbing and mechanical work. On a condominium above three storeys the structural scope requires a certified general contractor, because a building contractor licence is limited to structures of three storeys or fewer. Those are contracted by the association directly with the licensed party, and we sequence behind them.

One carve-out specific to tall buildings: among the few local licensing requirements preserved from the preemption is rooftop painting, coating and cleaning above three storeys, which a municipality may still licence where it did so before January 2021. On a mid-rise or high-rise, that is a question to settle before mobilising rather than after.

And the structural point that matters most on an old coastal building: where sounding or crack patterns suggest reinforcement corrosion or structural movement, that is an engineer’s finding, not a coating contractor’s. We report it, we stop, and we do not coat over it. A contractor willing to coat over it is selling you a shorter interval and a concealed problem.

Questions

What owners ask

Is exterior painting really a statutory reserve component?

Yes. Fla. Stat. 718.112(2)(g) names waterproofing and exterior painting among the components a structural integrity reserve study must cover, alongside the roof; the structure including load-bearing walls and primary structural members and systems; fireproofing and fire protection systems; plumbing; electrical systems; and windows and exterior doors. It also catches any other item with a deferred maintenance or replacement cost above $25,000 whose failure would negatively affect those listed. The study must state the component's remaining useful life and replacement cost and set a funding schedule reaching that cost by the end of that life.

Can owners vote to waive the exterior painting reserve?

For a unit-owner-controlled association required to obtain a structural integrity reserve study, no. For budgets adopted on or after 31 December 2024, members may not vote to waive or reduce reserves for the statutory components, and those funds may not be used for any other purpose. The narrow exception is a multicondominium association operating a Division-approved alternative funding method. Separately, through 31 December 2028, an association that completed a milestone inspection within the previous two calendar years may vote by a majority of total voting interests to pause or reduce contributions for up to two consecutive budgets to pay for the repairs that inspection identified, after which a reserve study must be performed before contributions resume.

How often does a South Florida building need repainting?

Less often than owners hope and more often than a generic reserve schedule assumes. A well-specified elastomeric or high-build acrylic system on sound, properly prepared stucco commonly gives eight to twelve years on a sheltered inland elevation. On a direct-exposure coastal elevation with salt aerosol, driving rain and high ultraviolet, five to eight years is more honest. The exposed south and west faces of a building frequently reach the end of their service life while the north face still looks new, which is why a single building-wide interval is usually wrong in both directions at once.

How long should sealant last?

A high-performance silicone or polyurethane sealant to ASTM C920, installed in a correctly designed joint, may give fifteen to twenty years. One installed over an undersized joint, without backer rod, or with three-sided adhesion will commonly fail in three to five years regardless of the product's quality, because the joint cannot accommodate movement. A large proportion of the joints on a thirty-year-old building fall into the second category, which is why sealant quantities should be surveyed rather than assumed.

What does an exterior coating programme cost?

The honest answer is that it depends overwhelmingly on substrate condition, and the spread between a sound building and a badly deteriorated one is wider than the spread between contractors. Coating a sound, previously well-coated wall is predictable. Coating a wall with delaminated stucco, failed previous elastomeric and twenty years of mismatched patches is substrate repair with a coating at the end, and the repair is most of the cost. Access geometry, sealant quantity, the specified system and film thickness, occupied-building logistics and whether the work is phased are the other drivers. We survey and quantify by elevation so a board can tender competitively on real quantities rather than anchor on a rate.

Can you coat over a previous elastomeric?

Only where it has retained adhesion, which must be tested rather than assumed. Where a previous elastomeric has delaminated, a new system applied over it inherits the old system's bond, which is to say none, and will fail as a sheet. Removal is unwelcome and expensive and is sometimes the only defensible answer. A bid that does not address existing coating condition on a building with visible coating failure has either not assessed it or has decided not to raise it.

Do you need a contractor licence for this work?

Painting, waterproofing, caulking, pressure washing, plastering, stuccoing and decorative stone, tile, marble, granite and terrazzo installation are among the job scopes Fla. Stat. 489.117(4)(a) places outside state contractor licensing, and the same provision bars a local government from requiring a state or local licence to obtain a permit for those scopes. It does not cover structural repair, roofing, window and door replacement, or electrical, plumbing and mechanical work. On a condominium above three storeys structural work requires a certified general contractor, which the association contracts directly. One local carve-out survives for rooftop painting, coating and cleaning above three storeys where a municipality licensed it before January 2021.

What if the survey finds something structural?

We report it and stop. Sounding that reveals widespread delamination, crack patterns suggesting movement, or rust staining and spalling that indicates reinforcement corrosion are engineering findings, not coating findings, and they belong with a licensed engineer and then a certified general contractor. Coating over them conceals a structural problem behind a new finish and shortens the life of the coating as well, because the underlying movement or corrosion continues. A contractor willing to proceed in those circumstances is selling a shorter interval and a hidden liability.
