Building here · Budget

What it costs to build on this coast

Every number you are about to read is a range, and the range is wide for a reason. A per-square-foot figure is an output of a finished project, not an input to a new one, and two houses at the same number can cost entirely different amounts of money. What follows is the honest version: where the market sits in 2026, what actually moves a budget here, and the costs particular to these towns that no national guide mentions.

First

Whose numbers these are

A word about sourcing, because it changes how much weight to put on anything below.

Builders who publish cost guides generally quote their own contracts. That is the most honest data anyone has, and it is also one firm’s book — their client mix, their spec level, their subcontractors, their overhead. It is a real number and it is not your number.

We are not going to quote you ours, for the straightforward reason that we do not build new houses. Amalfi looks after properties once they exist, and we represent owners through design and permitting. That makes us unusually well placed to tell you what a finished house costs to run, and it means the construction ranges below are drawn from what builders active in this market have published for 2026 rather than invented.

Treat them as a map of the territory. The purpose of a range is to tell you which conversation you are in — whether you are looking at a six-million-dollar project or an eighteen-million-dollar one — so that you can sanity-check the first builder who gives you a number. It is not a quote and nobody can give you one from a postcode and a square footage.

What we can do, free and in about an hour, is read the parcel: what the zoning allows, what the flood elevation requires, whether the town restricts construction seasonally, whether there is an architectural review board, and what that combination tends to do to a budget. That is the part that makes the difference between a range and an estimate.

What a published range cannot know

  • Your site — access, soil, demolition, what is under the slab
  • Your flood elevation and therefore your foundation
  • Whether the town has an architectural review board
  • The spec level you will actually choose once you see options
  • Your design team and how resolved the drawings are
  • Market conditions at the month you buy out the trades
  • How many changes you will make after the contract is signed

Where the market sits in 2026

$1,300–1,800+Oceanfront, and sites constrained by an overlay or a review board. Concentrated in Palm Beach County and the barrier-island towns.

Two adjustments on top of the bands, both published by builders working here. Palm Beach County runs roughly ten to twenty-five per cent above comparable Miami-Dade waterfront for the same house — a function of review boards, restricted working, access and the contractor pool willing to work inside those constraints. And the High Velocity Hurricane Zone adds something like eight to twelve per cent over a non-HVHZ market, which is why a house in Naples and the same house in Fort Lauderdale are not the same price.

Those two adjustments compound in the direction people least expect. The most expensive place to build on this coast is not Miami. It is a constrained barrier-island parcel in Palm Beach County, where the review board shapes the design, the ordinance limits when you may work, and the site has one road in.

Note what the bands exclude, because the exclusions are where budgets break. Land is separate and on these streets it is frequently the majority of the project. Design fees run several per cent of construction. Permits, impact fees, surveys, engineering, testing and inspections are soft costs. Furniture is not in there. Neither is landscape, which on a waterfront property commonly adds five to twelve per cent of the total on its own.

And demolition is its own line. A teardown is not free ground: the existing house has to come down, which on a pre-1978 structure means an asbestos survey and lead-safe practices, and the seawall and dock you inherited may need work before anything else can be financed.

Outside the per-square-foot number

  • Land
  • Demolition, and the surveys a pre-1978 teardown requires
  • Architectural and engineering fees
  • Permits, impact fees, surveys, testing and inspections
  • Landscape and hardscape — often five to twelve per cent of total
  • Pool, dock and seawall work
  • Furniture, art and window treatments
  • Carrying cost on the land and the construction loan
  • The contingency you will use

The levers

What actually moves the number

Square footage is the obvious one and it is not the interesting one. These are the decisions that change a budget by hundreds of thousands without changing the floor plan at all.

Glass

The single largest swing in a modern waterfront house. Steel and bronze window systems can add in the region of $150 to $300 per square foot over good aluminium, and on a house designed around the view that is a very large number. In the hurricane zone every unit also has to be impact-rated and product-approved, which narrows the field of suppliers and removes the cheap end of it.

The foundation and what is under it

Flood elevation decides how high the house sits, and height costs money — fill, retaining, stem walls or piles, longer utility runs, more stair. Soil decides whether you are on a conventional footing or on piles. Neither is visible in a floor plan and both are set before design begins.

Millwork and finishes

Custom cabinetry and architectural joinery on a large house commonly runs from several hundred thousand into seven figures. Stone selection does something similar on a smaller base. This is the category where an owner’s taste, discovered during design rather than before it, moves a budget most.

Systems

A full control, audio, network and shade package is a six-figure line on a substantial house and can run several hundred thousand. Generators, lifts, wine rooms and water treatment each add. None of it shows in the square footage and all of it shows in the invoice.

Site and access

A narrow barrier-island lot with one approach, no staging area and a town that requires police escorts for heavy vehicles is a materially more expensive build than the same house on an open mainland site. Access is priced into every single trade, quietly, and it is one of the first things we look at on a parcel.

Time

Carrying cost is a real construction cost and it is the one nobody puts in the spreadsheet. On a build that takes two years rather than eighteen months, the loan, the insurance and the rent somewhere else all run six months longer. Which brings us to the thing that is specific to these towns.

Local

The cost nobody writes down: the calendar

Several towns on this coast restrict when construction may happen, and the restrictions fall in the dry season when everyone wants to work. They lengthen programmes, and a longer programme is a more expensive one.

In Gulf Stream, between 1 December and 1 May, work runs weekdays only and the town prohibits pile driving, dredging, dry sandblasting, jackhammering and concrete mixing and pumping outright. You cannot pour a structure there in season. The ordinance even defines planting, pruning and mowing as regulated activity, so the landscape sits under the same clock.

In the Town of Palm Beach, the season runs roughly November to the end of April with weekday-only working, nothing at all between 24 December and 1 January, and section 42-198 barring dry sandblasters, pneumatic jackhammers, pile drivers and dredges in season and at weekends. In Highland Beach, Chapter 12 permits Saturdays only from nine to three, and even then prohibits concrete delivery and pumping, dump trucks, cranes and jackhammering.

What this does to a budget is simple and large. Structural work has to be sequenced into the summer, which means a build that misses its window waits for the next one. Trucks over a given weight need notice and sometimes a police escort. Contractors must register with several of these towns before starting. Deliveries are restricted to hours that do not match anybody’s supply chain. All of it is absorbed into trade pricing by builders who know the territory, and all of it surprises builders who do not.

This is the clearest reason a Palm Beach County waterfront house runs above a comparable Miami-Dade one. It is not that the materials cost more. It is that the calendar is shorter, the access is harder, and the pool of contractors who can work inside those rules without losing money is small.

Priced in by anyone who knows

  • Seasonal bans on structural and noisy work
  • Weekday-only working for five or six months of the year
  • Restricted arrival and departure times for every vehicle
  • Police escorts for heavy loads and all crane work
  • Staging areas away from the site, sometimes a mile away
  • Town contractor registration before the first day
  • Architectural or historic review on a monthly calendar
  • The risk of missing a pour window and waiting a season

The metric

Why price per square foot misleads

It is the first question every owner asks and the least informative answer anyone can give. Here is what it hides.

The denominator is negotiable. One builder counts conditioned space. Another counts under-roof area, which includes the loggia, the garage and the covered terrace. On a house with a great deal of outdoor living — which is most houses here — the second number can be twenty or thirty per cent larger, and the price per square foot falls accordingly while the cost does not change by a dollar.

The numerator is negotiable too. Does it include the pool, the dock, the landscape, the generator, the allowances? Does it include the builder’s fee and general conditions, or are those separate? Two bids at the same per-square-foot figure can be hundreds of thousands apart once you read what is in them.

The arithmetic also runs backwards from how people use it. A smaller house costs more per square foot, not less, because the kitchen, the bathrooms and the systems — the expensive rooms — are a larger share of a smaller plan. An owner who shrinks the house to hit a per-square-foot target usually finds the figure goes up.

A better question, and the one builders respect: what is your cost per habitable room at my spec level on a site like this, and what is in it. Followed by a request for the exclusions list. Exclusions are where arguments start, and a builder who hands them over without being chased is telling you something useful about how the job will run.

What to ask instead

  • What area are you measuring — conditioned, or under roof
  • Is the builder’s fee and general conditions inside this number
  • What allowances are carried, and at what level
  • Show me the exclusions list
  • What is the contingency, who holds it, and who benefits if it is unspent
  • What is in the schedule for this town’s seasonal restrictions
  • What happens to the price if the permit takes a round longer

After

The number nobody budgets: keeping it

We look after finished houses, so this is the part we actually know, and it is the line most owners discover in year three.

A waterfront house on this coast is a high-maintenance object and the climate is the reason. Airborne chloride attacks metal continuously, including stainless that was specified without regard to grade. Ultraviolet at this latitude retires coatings on the south and west elevations years before the north. Sealant joints go brittle and open before the paint looks tired. Mechanical plant works harder and longer than it would anywhere else in the country.

The practical effect is that items an owner expects to last a decade last half that on an exposed elevation, and the costly failures are the ones that went unnoticed: a joint that opened and let water into a wall, a fixing that corroded and split the cast stone around it, an air handler that ran dirty for two summers.

None of this is an argument against building. It is an argument for knowing the running figure before you set the construction budget, because the two trade against each other. The specification decisions that cost more at construction — 316 stainless rather than 304, vapour-open coatings on masonry that needs to breathe, non-staining sealants against stone, a sensible grade of exterior joinery — are the ones that reduce the thirty-year number most.

We are happy to review a specification from that angle while there is still time to change it, which is usually the most valuable hour anyone spends on a project and costs nothing.

Decisions that cut the running cost

  • Grade 316 stainless on anything within reach of salt air
  • Vapour-open coatings wherever the masonry needs to breathe
  • Non-staining sealants everywhere a joint meets stone
  • Mechanical plant sized and sited for service access
  • Fresh-water rinse points on the exposed elevations
  • Exterior joinery specified for the exposure it will actually face
  • A documented record of what was installed, before it is buried

Questions

What owners ask

How much does it cost to build a custom home in South Florida in 2026?

Hard construction cost runs roughly $450 to $650 per square foot for high-end production custom work on the mainland, $650 to $900 for genuinely bespoke custom, $900 to $1,300 on direct waterfront, and $1,300 to $1,800 or more on oceanfront and review-board-constrained sites. Palm Beach County runs about ten to twenty-five per cent above comparable Miami-Dade waterfront, and the High Velocity Hurricane Zone adds roughly eight to twelve per cent over markets outside it. Those are hard costs only — land, design fees, permits, landscape, pool, dock and furniture all sit outside them.

Why is Palm Beach County more expensive than Miami?

Three reasons that compound. Architectural and historic review boards shape the design and add months to approval. Several towns restrict construction seasonally — Gulf Stream prohibits concrete pumping and pile driving outright between December and May, and Palm Beach runs weekday-only working through its season — which shortens the working year and lengthens programmes. And barrier-island access is genuinely difficult, with restricted hours, off-site staging and police escorts for heavy vehicles. The pool of contractors who can work profitably inside those constraints is small, and the price reflects it.

What does the High Velocity Hurricane Zone add to the cost?

Published figures from builders working in it put it at roughly eight to twelve per cent over a comparable market outside the zone. The zone covers Miami-Dade and Broward counties. The cost is in product approval and testing requirements, higher design wind speeds, impact-rated glazing throughout, and a narrower field of approved products and suppliers — which removes the inexpensive end of several categories rather than simply adding a percentage to all of them.

Is a teardown cheaper than building on empty land?

Usually not, and sometimes considerably more expensive. Demolition is its own cost, and on a pre-1978 house it brings an asbestos survey and lead-safe work practices with it. You may inherit a seawall and a dock that need work, a non-conforming structure whose footprint you cannot rebuild to, and utilities in the wrong place. What a teardown buys is location and often an existing elevation and permit history, which can be worth all of it. It is simply not a discount.

How much should I allow for landscape?

On a waterfront property, commonly five to twelve per cent of total project cost, and it is almost never inside a construction price per square foot. Mature palms, specimen material, irrigation, lighting, hardscape, water features and the drainage underneath it add up quickly, and the landscape is also the first thing anyone sees. Budget it as a line rather than discovering it at the end, because it is the item most often cut to pay for overruns elsewhere, which is a false economy on a house of this kind.

What contingency should I carry?

Ten per cent of construction cost is the conventional answer and it is thin on a custom house on a difficult site. Fifteen is more realistic on waterfront, and twenty where the site is unknown, the drawings are not fully resolved at contract, or the design is still evolving. Ask who holds the contingency and what happens to it if it is unspent — the answer tells you a great deal about the contract you are being offered.

Can you give me a price?

No, and neither can anyone else from a postcode and a square footage. What we can do, without charge and in about an hour, is read the parcel: what the zoning permits, what the flood elevation will require of the foundation, whether the town restricts construction seasonally, whether an architectural review board is involved, and how that combination tends to affect both budget and programme. That turns a published range into something you can actually test a builder’s number against.

Do you build houses?

No. Amalfi looks after properties once they are finished and represents owners through design and permitting — reading the parcel, validating budgets, assembling the team and sitting on your side of the table. On new construction the house is built by a licensed general contractor, engaged by you. We are useful before that contractor is chosen and after they have gone, and we will tell you the truth about both because we are not bidding on the work.
