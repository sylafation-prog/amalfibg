Historic · Compliance

If your house predates 1978, the rules changed in 2010

Federal law requires that anyone paid to disturb paint on a home built before 1978 is a certified firm, using a certified individual, following specific containment and cleaning practices. In Florida it is administered by the EPA directly rather than by the state. Most owners of old houses have never heard of it, and a surprising number of contractors working on them are not certified.

The rule

What the Renovation, Repair and Painting rule actually requires

It applies to compensated work that disturbs painted surfaces in pre-1978 housing and child-occupied facilities. It is a work-practice rule, not a testing rule.

Lead was permitted in residential paint in the United States until 1978, so any house older than that is assumed to contain it unless testing shows otherwise. The hazard is not the paint sitting on the wall. It is the dust and chips created when somebody sands it, scrapes it, cuts into it, or hits it with water at pressure — which is exactly what maintenance and renovation do.

The rule therefore regulates the work rather than the building. A firm being paid to do that work must hold EPA firm certification, must assign a certified renovator to each job, must follow prescribed containment, cleaning and verification practices, must give the owner EPA’s lead pamphlet before work begins, and must keep records of all of it.

Florida does not run its own programme. A number of states are authorised to administer the rule themselves; Florida is not among them. That means your contractor is certified by the EPA, and the certificate you are looking at should say so.

The threshold

How much work triggers it

There is a minor repair and maintenance exemption, and it is small.

The practical reading for an owner of an older Florida house: assume the rule applies to essentially any real exterior work on your property, and treat the exemption as covering genuinely small repairs rather than as a loophole.

Six square feet inside

Interior work disturbing less than six square feet of painted surface in a room can fall under the minor repair exemption. That is about the size of a large pillow. A single window surround can exceed it.

Twenty square feet outside

Exterior work disturbing less than twenty square feet may fall under the exemption. Twenty square feet is roughly a four-by-five-foot patch. Washing, sanding or scraping one elevation of a house clears it in the first few minutes.

Windows are never minor

Window replacement is excluded from the exemption regardless of area, because of how much dust the removal of an old sash and frame generates. If somebody is replacing windows in a pre-1978 house, the rule applies, full stop.

In practice

What a certified firm has to do on your property

  • Give you the pamphlet first. EPA’s Renovate Right booklet, before work starts, with a signed acknowledgement kept on file. This is the step most often skipped, and its absence is the easiest thing for an owner to notice.
  • Assign a certified renovator to the job. Not to the company — to the job. That person is responsible for the work practices on site and for training any non-certified workers who disturb paint.
  • Contain the work area. Plastic sheeting on the ground and over openings, a defined perimeter, and for exterior work an exclusion zone sized to the height of the work, with everything within it covered.
  • Prohibit the worst methods. Open-flame burning, torching, machine sanding or grinding without HEPA extraction, and heat guns above a set temperature are not permitted on lead-based paint.
  • Clean and verify. HEPA vacuuming and wet cleaning, then a cleaning verification procedure performed by the certified renovator before the containment comes down.
  • Keep the records for three years and make them available on request.

Why it is your problem

What an uncertified crew actually costs you

The containment and cleaning requirements are not paperwork. The dust from sanding or washing old exterior paint settles on soil, terraces, planting, pool surrounds and the inside of a house through open windows, and it stays there. If the house has children or grandchildren in it, that is the entire point of the rule. If it does not, the soil contamination is still yours to live with and to disclose.

There is a commercial dimension too. A firm that is not certified is working outside a federal rule on your property, which is not a position an owner of a significant house wants to be in, and it tends to correlate with the other corners being cut — the pressure too high, the chemical wrong, the clean-up partial.

And it is a simple quality signal. Certification costs a firm a few hundred dollars and a day of training. A contractor who has not bothered, on a street full of pre-war houses, is telling you something.

Checking

How to verify a contractor in two minutes

There are two certificates and you should ask to see both, because they are different things.

The firm certificate

Issued by EPA to the company, valid for five years, and carrying the firm’s name and a certification number. Ask for a copy. Check that the name on it matches the name on your contract and that the date has not run out.

The renovator certificate

Issued to an individual who has completed the accredited course. Ask which certified renovator is assigned to your job, by name, and ask to see theirs. A firm certificate with nobody certified on site is not compliance.

Or call and ask

EPA’s National Lead Information Center, on 1-800-424-LEAD, will confirm certification and will also tell you where accredited training is offered, which is a reasonable way to check a claim you have been given verbally.

Washing

Where exterior cleaning sits in all this

Pressure washing painted pre-1978 siding, trim or render is precisely the kind of paint disturbance the rule contemplates, and it very easily exceeds twenty square feet. The water carries the paint it removes into the soil and across whatever is downwind, and on a waterfront property a good deal of that is the seawall and the water beyond it.

On a historic house there is a second reason for restraint that has nothing to do with lead: high pressure destroys lime render, erodes soft mortar joints and furrows old timber permanently. The two arguments point in the same direction, which is low pressure, appropriate chemistry, containment and a controlled rinse rather than a man with a four-thousand-pound machine and an afternoon.

Nothing here is legal advice and the EPA is the authority on its own rule. The thresholds, the exemptions and the required practices are published, they change, and a specific property may have been tested and cleared. Ask the agency, or ask a certified firm to walk you through what applies to yours.

Questions

Quick answers

Does the lead rule apply to my house?

If it was built before 1978 and somebody is being paid to disturb painted surfaces on it, almost certainly yes. Lead was permitted in residential paint in the United States until 1978, so older housing is assumed to contain it unless testing shows otherwise. The rule covers pre-1978 housing and child-occupied facilities, and it regulates the work rather than the building.

How much work has to be happening before the rule kicks in?

The minor repair and maintenance exemption covers interior work disturbing less than six square feet of painted surface per room, or exterior work disturbing less than twenty square feet. Window replacement is never treated as minor regardless of area. Twenty square feet is a patch roughly four feet by five, so any substantial exterior work on a house clears the threshold almost immediately.

Who enforces this in Florida?

The EPA directly. A number of states are authorised to run their own version of the programme; Florida is not one of them, so Florida contractors are certified by EPA and their certificates say so. EPA’s National Lead Information Center, on 1-800-424-LEAD, answers questions and can confirm certification.

What should I ask a contractor before they start?

For a copy of the firm’s EPA certificate, the name of the certified renovator assigned to your job and a copy of their individual certificate, and for the Renovate Right pamphlet with an acknowledgement for you to sign. If any of those three produce a blank look, that is your answer.

Does pressure washing count as disturbing paint?

It readily can. Washing painted pre-1978 siding, trim or render at pressure removes paint, and the water carries it into the soil and across the surroundings. It also exceeds twenty square feet almost at once. On a historic house there is a second reason to work gently in any case: high pressure erodes lime render, opens soft mortar joints and permanently furrows old timber.

Do I have to have my house tested for lead?

No. The rule does not require an owner to test. A certified firm may either assume lead is present and follow the work practices, or have the surfaces tested by an appropriate method and work outside the rule if they are shown to be clear. Testing can be worth doing on a large project, because it may take the whole job out of scope, but it is a choice rather than an obligation.
