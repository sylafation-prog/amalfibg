Estates · While you are away

One steward, a written calendar, and a record

Every firm in this business promises documentation. Almost none of them will show you what their documentation looks like, which is revealing, because the record is the entire product. A visit you cannot inspect afterwards is a visit you have to take on trust, and trust is not what an insurer accepts, or a buyer, or a claims adjuster eighteen months later.

The record

The fixed positions matter more than the number of photographs. Fifty pictures taken from wherever the steward happened to be standing prove that someone was there. Eleven taken from the same eleven positions every visit, for three years, show a hairline crack lengthening, a stain spreading, a tile lifting at one corner. One is evidence of attendance. The other is evidence of condition, and only the second is worth anything to an insurer or a buyer.

What fails

What actually goes wrong in an empty house here, in cost order

The calendar is not a list of tasks. It is this list, with an interval attached to each line.

A supply line or water heater, running unattended

The catastrophic one, and the reason the main shutoff position and the heater's age are logged on every visit rather than noted once. A braided line fails, and in an occupied house someone hears it within minutes. In an empty house it runs until somebody arrives. Everything downstream — floors, cabinetry, plaster, wiring — is a function of how many days that was.

Air conditioning, and the humidity behind it

A failure does not announce itself in an empty house. Within days the relative humidity climbs, and in this climate that is the beginning of mould on soft furnishings, warping in cabinetry and millwork, and damage to anything with a hide or a soundboard. Which is why humidity is read and recorded rather than assumed from the fact that the unit is running.

A tripped breaker

Trivial, until you work out what was on the circuit. A freezer, a wine room, a fish tank, a dehumidifier in a basement store. The panel is walked on every visit for exactly this reason, and it takes ninety seconds.

Irrigation, running unseen

Nobody is home to notice that a head has shifted and is now watering the glass instead of the bed. Six months of daily hard water on a window in the sun is how permanent etching happens, and the same water is attacking the stucco and the stone beside it.

The pool, left to itself

Chemistry drifts, algae establishes, and the finish stains in a way that does not come back out with a brush. Aggressive water also attacks plaster, grout and coping from the other direction. Weekly in season is not a convention; it is the interval the chemistry requires.

Salt, with nobody to rinse it

An onshore blow coats every surface in chloride aerosol, and salt is hygroscopic — it pulls moisture from humid air and holds a conductive film on metal long after the weather clears. So corrosion continues in dry sunshine. A fresh-water rinse after the blow, not after the season, is what stops it.

The cameras

Why recording is checked by playback and not by a live view

This is the single most common false assurance in an empty house, and it takes two minutes a visit to disprove.

A live view proves exactly one thing: that the camera has power and a network path to the person looking. It proves nothing whatever about whether anything is being retained. And every one of the common failures presents as a perfectly healthy live picture.

The recorder's storage fills and the overwrite policy silently drops footage faster than the retention you were told. A drive fails or drops out of the array while the camera carries on streaming. A recording schedule or motion zone gets edited — often by an installer during an unrelated visit — so the camera streams but records nothing, or records only a zone that excludes the approach to the house. The recorder is capturing a low-resolution substream while the live view shows you the high-resolution main stream, so what is retained is useless for identifying anyone. The clock or timezone has drifted, so retained footage cannot be found by timestamp when it is wanted.

Not one of those is visible from a live tile. The only test that discriminates is to take a timestamp from the previous visit, scrub back to it, and confirm an identifiable image exists at the resolution you expect. That is what we do, and we record the retention depth we found while we are there.

It is a small thing that turns out to matter enormously at exactly the moment nothing else helps. An owner discovers a problem on a Tuesday, asks for the footage from three weeks earlier, and learns then that the system has been recording nothing since an installer changed a setting in March.

Verified on every visit

  • Playback at a known timestamp from the previous visit
  • Retention depth confirmed and recorded
  • Resolution of the retained stream, not the live one
  • Clock and timezone checked against actual time
  • Motion zones still covering the approaches
  • Alarm state, and whether it was armed on arrival
  • Gates cycled and observed, not just signalled

Insurance

The visit record is often a policy requirement, not a convenience

Worth checking your own policy before you decide how often a house should be looked at.

Many carriers impose conditions on a dwelling left unoccupied for an extended period, and periodic inspection at a stated interval is a common one. Where that condition exists, the documented visit record is the thing that demonstrates compliance — and the absence of one is the thing an adjuster reaches for when a claim arrives after a long absence.

We are not insurance advisers and we will not tell you what your policy says. What we will do is read the relevant condition with you before the calendar is written, so the interval we work to is the interval your policy requires rather than the one that happens to suit a service plan. If your carrier wants fortnightly and the firm you are speaking to sells monthly, that is worth discovering now.

The same record does other work later. It is what supports a storm claim by establishing the condition of the property beforehand, in dated photographs from fixed positions. It is what distinguishes genuine storm damage from deferred maintenance, which is the distinction adjusters are trained to make and owners are rarely prepared for. And at sale it is a file a buyer's inspector can read, which changes a negotiation.

None of that works if the record is a checklist of ticks. It works because the photographs are dated and repeatable and the readings are numbers.

What to establish before writing the calendar

  • Whether your policy has an unoccupancy condition, and its interval
  • What the policy requires to be shut off, and when
  • Whether a water-leak detection device is required or discounted
  • What evidence the carrier expects after a named storm
  • Who the carrier wants notified, and how quickly
  • Whether the record format satisfies them — ask them, in writing

What this is not

Care, not agency, and the distinction protects you

Three words get used interchangeably in this market and they describe three different products with three different legal positions.

Estate care, which is this

  • Looking after a building and the things in it
  • A written standard, an interval, and a record against both
  • One named steward, and a single monthly statement
  • Trades scheduled into the calendar, contracting directly with you
  • No part in letting, marketing or collecting rent
  • No commission from any vendor

Property management, which is not

  • Letting the property, marketing it, collecting rent
  • Under chapter 475 that is licensed activity in Florida
  • The exemptions are narrow and specific — none covers a firm
  • renting a single-family estate on an owner's behalf
  • A firm doing both needs a brokerage licence
  • Worth asking any firm that offers both which one it holds

The third word is estate management, which in practice usually means staffing — an estate manager or house manager on an owner's payroll, often placed through an agency. That is a person, and it is a reasonable thing to want. It is not what this is. This is a firm, with a standard, a calendar and a record that survives a change of staff, a change of principal, or a sale. And on the question of credentials: Florida does not license home watch at all. The accreditation some firms advertise is a private trade credential rather than a state registration, which is worth knowing when it is presented as though it were a licence.

Questions

What owners ask

What should a home watch visit actually include?

Ask to see a specimen record before you ask about price, because the record is the product. Ours carries arrival and departure timestamps with the steward named; interior temperature and relative humidity by zone; the water main position, heater age and meter reading; mechanical running state, filter and condensate; the electrical panel walked for tripped breakers; pool chemistry read and logged; irrigation zones observed running with overspray noted; alarm state and cameras verified by playback; the exterior and grounds; dated photographs from fixed positions; and an exceptions list with recommendations. A firm that will not show you their format is asking you to take the visits on trust.

How often should an empty house be checked in Florida?

Start with your insurance policy rather than with a service plan, because many carriers impose conditions on a dwelling left unoccupied and a stated inspection interval is a common one. Beyond that, the interval follows the risks: pool chemistry wants weekly in season, humidity and mechanical want eyes on them frequently enough that a failure is caught in days rather than weeks, and the exterior can run longer. We would rather write the calendar around what your policy requires and what the property actually needs than sell you a frequency.

What goes wrong first in an unoccupied South Florida house?

In order of what it costs: a supply line or water heater failing and running unattended, which is catastrophic and is a function purely of how many days pass before someone arrives; air conditioning failing and the humidity that follows within days, which is where mould and damage to cabinetry, millwork and instruments comes from; a tripped breaker killing a freezer or a wine room; irrigation drifting and spraying the glass and stucco unseen; a pool going to algae and staining the finish permanently; and salt-laden wind with nobody there to rinse it off.

Why check camera recording by playback instead of looking at the live view?

Because a live view proves only that the camera has power and a network path. It cannot show you that storage has filled and is overwriting faster than the retention you were promised, that a drive has failed while the camera still streams, that a schedule or motion zone was edited by an installer so nothing is recorded, that the recorder is keeping a low-resolution substream while you are watching the high-resolution one, or that the clock has drifted so footage cannot be found by timestamp. Every one of those looks perfectly healthy live. The only test that discriminates is to scrub back to a known time from the previous visit and confirm a usable image exists.

Is home watch licensed in Florida?

No. There is no state licence, certification or registration for home watch in Florida. Some firms hold a private trade accreditation, which is a reasonable thing to have and is not a licence, so be careful when it is presented as though it were one. What is licensed is anything that strays into letting or managing property for an owner under chapter 475, and into the licensed building trades under chapter 489 — which is why the trades we schedule contract directly with you rather than through us.

What is the difference between home watch and property management?

Property management, in the sense the law uses, means letting the property, marketing it and collecting rent, and in Florida that is licensed activity under chapter 475 with narrow and specific exemptions — none of which covers a firm renting a single-family estate on an owner's behalf. Estate care is looking after the building and the things in it against a written standard, with no part in any transaction and no commission from any vendor. If a firm offers you both, it is reasonable to ask which licence it holds.

Will the record help with an insurance claim?

It is what establishes the condition of the property before the event, in dated photographs taken from the same positions over time. That distinction — genuine storm damage against deferred maintenance — is the one adjusters are trained to make and the one owners are least prepared for. It also demonstrates compliance where a policy imposes an inspection condition during unoccupancy. We are not insurance advisers; we read the relevant condition with you before the calendar is written so that the interval matches what your carrier actually asks for.

Can you look after the house while we are out of the country for months?

That is the usual arrangement rather than the exception, and the calendar is built backwards from your arrival date so the property is right on the day you land rather than being attended to afterwards. One named steward holds the house, the record goes to you after every visit whether or not anything happened, and anything outside the standard arrives as an exception with a recommendation rather than as a surprise when you walk in.
