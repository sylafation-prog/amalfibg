Estates · Programme X

Hard water does not sit on the glass. It eats it.

Everyone calls them water spots, which is why they get left. A deposit sitting on a surface can wait. What is actually happening on a South Florida window is a chemical attack on the glass itself, it runs on a timer, and there is a point past which no amount of polishing brings the glass back. Knowing which side of that point a window is on is the whole of this work.

The mechanism

What is actually happening to the glass

Not a deposit. An ion exchange, then an alkaline attack on the silica network, and the second one is irreversible.

Glass in contact with water undergoes ion exchange. The alkalis and alkaline earths in the glass — sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium — swap places with hydrogen ions from the water. The process is diffusion-controlled, which means it works inward from the surface, and it affects a layer on the order of a few microns. Critically, at this first stage the silicon network itself is not removed. What is left behind is a hydrated, silica-rich gel layer, and the reprecipitated silica and salts in it are what you see as dulling and translucency.

If it stopped there, every etched window in Florida would be recoverable. It does not stop there, and the reason is that the leaching changes the chemistry of the water sitting on the glass. Alkali hydroxides released out of the glass raise the local pH of that thin surface film above about nine. Hydroxide at that concentration attacks the silicon-oxygen bonds directly, converting them to silanol groups and opening the network. Water and hydroxide then penetrate the opened structure, a short hydration period forms isolated fissures, and a pit development period follows in which the basic species widen those fissures and dissolve the network locally.

The distinction that matters to an owner is simple to state and expensive to learn late. In the first stage material has been deposited on the glass and the glass has been depleted. In the second, glass has been removed. There is nothing to clean off a pit, because a pit is an absence.

The pH dependence is also why the problem accelerates rather than creeping along at a steady rate. Around pH seven to ten silica forms alternating-density layers; above pH ten it dissolves rapidly. Each cycle of wetting and evaporation leaves the surface slightly more alkaline than the last, so the attack gets faster as it goes. A window that looked tolerable last season is not on the same trajectory it was on.

Why it happens faster here

  • Hard municipal and well water, high in calcium and bicarbonate
  • Heat and sun, so droplets evaporate and concentrate rather than run off
  • Salt aerosol adding ions to the film
  • Humidity keeping a conductive film on the surface between wettings
  • Irrigation running daily, on a timer, whether anyone is home or not
  • Large single panes, so the cost of being wrong is high
  • Houses left unoccupied for months with nobody watching the spray

The diagnosis

Three stages, and only the middle one polishes out

This is the question every owner asks and almost every company dodges, because the honest answer sometimes costs them the job.

StageWhat you seeWhat it isWhat recovers it
OneHaze that disappears after washing and returnsA mineral deposit sitting on the surfaceWashing, or a mild acid. No polishing
TwoHaze that remains after washing; fine crazingThe leached, hydrated layer — glass depleted a few microns deepCerium oxide polishing, which removes microns. The scale matches
ThreeVisible pitting; loss of optical clarityThe silica network dissolved locally. Glass is goneNothing. Replacement is the honest answer

The reason cerium oxide works at stage two is quantitative rather than magical: the altered layer is a few microns deep and the polish removes a few microns. The reason it fails at stage three is geometric, not chemical. A pit is deeper than the polishable layer and has irregular walls, so polishing removes the sound glass around the pit faster than it reaches the pit floor. What you get is a lens — a shallow depression with optical distortion — and the pit still there. You do not polish a pit out. You thin the glass around it and make the window worse. Any company willing to attempt it on a stage three pane should be asked what they expect the result to look like from inside at night.

The cause

It is almost always the irrigation, and that is good news

On a waterfront estate the dominant driver is not rain and not sea spray. It is the sprinkler aimed slightly too high, running at five in the morning.

Follow the mechanism backwards and it leads somewhere specific. A droplet of hard water lands on the glass. The sun comes up and it evaporates, concentrating the calcium and bicarbonate it was carrying into a smaller and smaller volume. The local pH of that shrinking film climbs past nine. Hydroxide begins attacking the silicon-oxygen network. Then tomorrow it happens again, and the day after, on a controller nobody has looked at since the system was commissioned.

Rain does not do this, because rainwater is soft and it runs off rather than evaporating in place. Sea spray contributes, but intermittently and without the daily repetition. Irrigation overspray does it on a schedule, in the heat, to the same panes, for years.

Which means the single highest-value intervention on an etched elevation is not polishing at all. It is aiming the heads off the glass, and then off the stucco and the metalwork and the stone, all of which are being attacked by the same water for related reasons. Polishing a window that is still being sprayed every morning is a service you will be buying again.

This is also the clearest argument we can make for holding programmes on one calendar rather than buying them separately. The irrigation contractor is not asked to look at the windows. The window company is not asked to look at the sprinklers. Nobody is accountable for the seam between them, and the seam is where this damage comes from. We cover the irrigation side of it separately, where overspray is treated as building protection rather than a lawn question.

The first thing we do on a house with etched glass is walk the zones at the time they actually run, with the controller in hand, and photograph where the water lands. It is an unglamorous hour and it is worth more than the polishing.

What the walk establishes

  • Which heads are wetting glass, stone or metal, and at what time
  • Whether the controller schedule matches what is actually happening
  • Water source and hardness — municipal, well or a mix
  • Whether a softener or filter exists and is working
  • Which elevations take the morning sun, and therefore evaporate fastest
  • The stage of etching on each elevation, photographed and graded
  • Whether any pane has already passed the point of recovery

Protection

The coating, and why it is read rather than scheduled

A hydrophobic coating is not a wax and not a warranty. It is a measurable surface property that degrades, and the measurement is something an owner can watch.

A hydrophobic coating works by bonding low-energy groups to the glass through silicon-oxygen linkages, presenting a surface that water will not spread across. The technical definition of hydrophobic is a static water contact angle greater than ninety degrees; superhydrophobic is conventionally above a hundred and fifty. A fresh silane or siloxane coating sits comfortably above ninety, which is why water pulls into tight beads and sheds rather than lying on the surface.

Abrasion, ultraviolet light and alkaline cleaners progressively remove those bonded groups. As they go, the contact angle falls. Once it drops below about ninety degrees the surface is no longer hydrophobic, and water stops beading and starts sheeting — which matters enormously, because a sheet of water is exactly the thin evaporating film that starts the etching cascade described above. The coating failing is not a cosmetic event. It is the moment the clock on stage one restarts.

That is why the water-bead test is read and recorded at every visit rather than trusted to a reapplication calendar. It is a field proxy for contact angle: water flicked onto the pane either pulls into tight beads that run off, or it spreads and clings. The first is a working coating. The second is a window that needs recoating now, whatever the schedule says.

We will not publish a service life in months, because it is not a property of coatings in general — it belongs to the specific product, its data sheet and the exposure of the elevation. A north-facing pane behind a deep overhang and a west elevation taking afternoon sun and salt wind are not on the same cycle, and a company quoting you one interval for the whole house is quoting a convenience rather than a measurement.

Recorded at every visit

  • Water-bead test per elevation, read and logged
  • Where beading has failed, and on which panes
  • Etching stage per elevation, photographed from fixed positions
  • Any change since the previous visit
  • Whether irrigation has been adjusted since last noted
  • Film condition, edges and any lifting
  • Recoat recommended, with the elevation and the reason

The water

Purified water, and why it is not a gimmick

The same chemistry that explains the damage explains the washing method. Water with nothing dissolved in it cannot leave anything behind.

Ordinary tap water on this coast carries a substantial dissolved mineral load. Wash a window with it and, however well the glass is squeegeed, whatever film remains dries and leaves that load on the surface — which is the first stage of the process described at the top of this page. Cleaning a window with hard water is, in a small way, contributing to the problem it is meant to fix.

Our van carries a purification system that strips the dissolved solids out before the water reaches the glass. Water in that state has nothing to deposit. It dries without residue, so the panes can be rinsed and left rather than squeegeed dry, which also means frames, tracks and sills get properly flushed rather than wiped around — and the tracks and sills are where the salt and grit that scratch glass actually sit.

It matters more on an estate than on a suburban house for an unremarkable reason: the panes are enormous, often fixed, and frequently at height. Anything requiring a squeegee and a ladder on every pane is a cost that scales badly and a risk that scales worse. Rinsing at reach is both safer and better for the glass.

The same principle governs the rest of the envelope, and it is mostly a principle of restraint. Water at the pressure used on a driveway will clean a wall and will also take the surface off lime stucco, cast stone or aged coquina, and that damage does not grow back. Washing is a technique inside the calendar rather than a service we sell, and on a good building the question is always how little will do it. We have written about what pressure actually does to an older building separately.

What the glass programme covers

  • Purified water rinse, leaving no mineral residue

Two cautions

Things worth saying that cost us work

Both of these are reasons to turn a job down, and both are reasons to trust whoever tells you about them.

Tempered glass is not ordinary glass

  • Its surface is in compression by design, which is what makes it strong
  • Removing a few microns in a polish is immaterial to that
  • Aggressive grinding into the compressive layer is a different matter
  • Tempered glass does not chip or craze when it fails — it fails all at once
  • So the restraint matters more than the technique
  • On tempered panes we will stop short rather than chase the last of a stage two haze
  • And we will say that before starting, not afterwards

Film is not always just film

  • Ultraviolet and solar film reduces heat gain and fading, and is uncontroversial
  • Security laminate is a different conversation
  • Film specified or marketed as impact protection becomes a windstorm protective device
  • That brings product approval into it, and in Broward a licensed installer
  • We will fit UV, solar and privacy film
  • We will not represent any film as hurricane protection
  • If opening protection is what you want, that is a separate job with an approval behind it

The second one is worth dwelling on, because it is sold loosely. A security laminate makes a pane harder to break through, which is a genuine benefit against intrusion. It is not the same thing as an opening protection system tested to the impact and cyclic pressure protocols, and in the high-velocity hurricane zone the difference is not a matter of degree. An owner told otherwise should ask for the product approval number, and should get one.

Questions

What owners ask

Can hard water stains be removed from glass?

It depends which of three stages the glass is in, and that is a diagnosis rather than a sales question. If the haze disappears when the pane is washed and returns later, it is a mineral deposit sitting on the surface and washing or a mild acid handles it. If the haze remains after washing and you can see fine crazing, the glass itself has been leached a few microns deep — that is the stage cerium oxide polishing genuinely recovers, because the polish removes roughly the depth that was altered. If the pane is visibly pitted, the silica network has dissolved locally and glass is gone. Nothing recovers that, and anyone who says otherwise is proposing to thin the glass around the pit.

Why does polishing not work on pitted glass?

The reason is geometric rather than chemical. A pit is deeper than the layer polishing can remove, and its walls are irregular. Polishing therefore takes away the sound glass surrounding the pit faster than it reaches the pit floor, so you end up with a shallow lens-shaped depression with optical distortion in it, and the pit still present. The window is worse, not better, and the distortion is most obvious at night from inside. We would rather tell you the pane needs replacing than sell you that.

What causes etched glass on a Florida house?

Almost always irrigation overspray, and the mechanism is specific. A droplet of hard water lands on the pane, the sun evaporates it, and the calcium and bicarbonate it carried are concentrated into a shrinking film. That raises the local pH above about nine, at which point hydroxide starts attacking the silicon-oxygen bonds in the glass. Then it repeats tomorrow, on a timer. Rain does not do this because rainwater is soft and runs off; sea spray contributes but not daily. Which is why the first fix is aiming the heads, not polishing the glass — polishing a pane that is still sprayed every morning is work you will buy again.

What is the water-bead test and why does it matter?

It is a field reading of the contact angle on a coated pane. A hydrophobic surface is defined by a static water contact angle above ninety degrees, and a fresh silane or siloxane coating sits well above that, so water pulls into tight beads and sheds. As abrasion, ultraviolet and alkaline cleaners strip the bonded groups, the angle falls, and once it drops below about ninety the water stops beading and starts sheeting. Sheeting water is precisely the thin evaporating film that starts the etching. So the bead test is not decoration — the moment beading fails is the moment protection has ended, and that is when a recoat is due regardless of what the calendar says.

How often should a hydrophobic coating be renewed?

We will not quote you a number of months, and be a little wary of anyone who does for a whole house. Service life belongs to the specific product and to the exposure of the particular elevation. A sheltered north-facing pane and a west elevation taking afternoon sun and salt wind are not on the same cycle. The coating is renewed when the bead test says it has failed on that elevation, which is why the test is read and recorded at every visit rather than trusted to a schedule.

Why use purified water to clean windows?

Because ordinary water on this coast carries a heavy dissolved mineral load, and whatever film is left after cleaning dries and deposits that load back onto the glass — which is the first stage of the etching process. Water with the dissolved solids stripped out has nothing to leave behind, so panes can be rinsed and left to dry without residue. On an estate it also means the frames, tracks and sills get flushed rather than wiped around, and it removes the need to squeegee every pane by hand, which on large fixed glass at height is both the slow part and the risky part.

Is restoring glass safe on tempered panes?

Tempered glass has a surface in permanent compression, which is what gives it its strength, and removing a few microns in a polish is immaterial to that. Grinding aggressively into the compressive layer is a different matter, and tempered glass does not fail gradually — it goes all at once. So on tempered panes the restraint matters more than the technique, and we will stop short of chasing the last of a stubborn haze rather than push it. We say that before starting rather than afterwards.

Can window film be used as hurricane protection?

Ultraviolet, solar and privacy films are straightforward and we fit them. Security laminate is a different conversation: film specified or marketed as impact protection becomes a windstorm protective device, which brings product approval into it and, in Broward, a licensed installer. Making a pane harder to break through is a real benefit against intrusion, and it is not the same thing as an opening protection system tested to the impact and cyclic pressure protocols. If anyone tells you otherwise, ask for the product approval number.
