The house

Finished means finished.

Amalfi was founded by a European-born operator who spent twenty years owning and managing commercial buildings, and renovating them with his own hands rather than hiring someone to hold the schedule for him.

It is independently owned. Not a franchise, not a branch of anything, and not a call centre. The person whose name is on it answers for the work.

Why this exists

From the other side of the table

Most contractors have never been the client. They have not waited three days for a call back about a unit that failed on a Friday, or paid for the same repair twice because nobody wrote down what was done the first time, or discovered a change order on an invoice rather than before the work.

We have. Amalfi was built by someone who spent two decades on the owner's side — budgeting for equipment replacement, chasing vendors who did not arrive, and running his own renovations because the alternative was worse. Every commitment on this page exists because its absence cost us something.

We also come out of a trade where nothing moves without a photograph and a signature, and where the file is as much the work as the repair. Houses get the same treatment here. That is not a feature we added to look thorough — it is the only way we know how to work.

That is also why the three divisions sit under one roof. A property does not experience its problems by trade. It experiences them as a list of people who need to be called, and nobody who is answerable for the whole of it.

Three commitments

What you can hold us to

Written before it begins

Every property receives an assessment and a schedule in writing before a crew is sent. You know what is to be done, when, and at what cost. Changes are agreed in advance, never discovered afterwards.

Craft

A European standard of finish

We talk about edges, tolerances, surface preparation and how many coats because those are the things that decide whether work lasts two years or seven. On this coast, where salt and sun shorten every cycle, preparation is not a detail. It is the whole job.

The founder is European-born. What came over with him is not a trade certificate — it is a set of expectations about how a building is put together, and a low tolerance for the habits that this market has come to treat as normal. Four of those expectations do most of the work.

In practice

What the European standard actually means

Said plainly, because the phrase is usually used as decoration. These are the four differences that show up in the work and on the invoice.

  1. How a tradesman is madeIn Germany, Austria, Switzerland and much of Italy a trade is entered through a formal apprenticeship of three or four years, examined at the end, and only then worked at unsupervised. The senior grade — Meister, maître, maestro — is not a marketing title; it is a qualification to train others and to sign for work. The American pattern is mostly on the job: a willing man is handed a tool and learns from whoever is standing nearest. Both produce excellent tradesmen. Only one of them produces a floor you can assume is there. We do not assume it — every person on a crew is worked alongside and checked on our own jobs before being left alone on a client's property.
  2. Tolerance is a number, not an opinionA terrace is flat or it is not, and the difference is measurable. Lippage between tiles, deviation under a two-metre straightedge, the fall across a terrace towards its drains, the gap at a door reveal, the plumb of a render stop — these go into the scope in millimetres before material is ordered, and they are checked with a level and a straightedge rather than judged by eye once the scaffold is down. Work that can be measured rarely has to be argued about.
  3. Specified for a service life, not a warranty periodThe convention here is to specify to the warranty. A coating carries five years, so five years is what gets bought, and the next cycle is somebody else's problem. The European habit is to ask how long the assembly is meant to last and then buy the thing that lasts it — heavier stock, better fixings, a bedding mortar that moves with the substrate instead of cracking off it, 316 stainless rather than 304 within sight of the water. On a seafront elevation that costs more at the start and appreciably less across fifteen years. We will quote both and show you the arithmetic rather than making the choice for you.
  4. Detailed so it can be maintainedAn old villa is full of details that assume somebody will come back: a valve you can reach without opening a wall, a pump that lifts out without breaking tile, coping bedded so it can be raised rather than chiselled off, a movement joint where the building actually moves. A great deal of modern work assumes the opposite — that the assembly will be demolished before it is ever serviced. We detail for the first case. It is the difference between a repair that takes an afternoon and one that takes a fortnight and a tenant's patience.

The parallel

The same sea, with a longer argument behind it

There is nothing sentimental in the name. South Florida and the Amalfi Coast are the same building problem in two languages. Both are hot and humid for most of the year. Both take salt carried inland on the wind, which drives chloride into reinforcement, fixings and condenser coils alike. Both sit under a sun that breaks down organic coatings and polymer sealants far faster than any northern climate. And both build in masonry and render rather than timber, so both move, crack and need re-pointing rather than replacing.

The difference is how long each coast has had to argue about it. Positano, Amalfi and Ravello have been rendering, bedding, re-pointing and patching the same walls for centuries. The answers that survived did so because the alternatives failed in public view: lime renders that let a wall breathe instead of trapping moisture behind it, generous overhangs that keep weather off an elevation, stone set so it can be lifted and re-laid, bronze and marine-grade stainless instead of plated steel, drainage resolved before anything decorative is chosen.

Coastal Florida has been building at this scale for roughly seventy years. A great deal of what will eventually fail here is failing for the first time right now, and the market is learning the lesson at full price. Borrowing the answer is simply cheaper than discovering it.

So when we put a movement joint where a mastic would have been faster, or hold a terrace until its fall is right rather than tiling over a flat slab and selling you a drain later, it is not taste. It is the shorter route to a result that somebody else already paid to learn.

The consequence

What that is worth to you

A standard that stays on a website is worth nothing. Here is where it reaches you.

A scope you can measure against

Materials by grade, fixings by alloy, tolerances in millimetres, coats and film thickness, surface preparation named. If the finished work does not meet the number, there is nothing to debate — it is put right.

Two prices and the arithmetic

Where a longer-lived specification costs more at the outset, you receive both figures and the comparison across the life of the assembly. The cheaper option is always available to you. It is just never quoted silently.

A building that can be serviced

Access, isolation and lift-out designed in, and a closeout file recording what was used, where it came from and what will need attention in which year. The next repair does not begin with a demolition.
